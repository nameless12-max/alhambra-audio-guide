# title: Stop 1. Elvira del Campo: the woman who would not eat pork
# stop: 1
# room: Anywhere

## Two men in the house

N: A summer day, in a village south of Toledo. A notary's wife is arrested and brought to the city. Her name is Elvira del Campo. She is pregnant. She is a Christian, baptised, a churchgoer. But her family were Jewish, generations ago.

N: The charge? Two men who lived in her own house have spoken to the Inquisition. They say she does not eat pork. And that she puts on clean linen on Saturdays, the Jewish sabbath.

N: That is all. A plate pushed away. A fresh shirt on the wrong day.

...

N: Now here is the first thing that makes this court so frightening. Elvira is never told who accused her. She is not told exactly what they said, or when. She has to guess.

N: So she guesses. She lists everyone she thinks might hate her. Twelve names. The judges agree to strike out two. One of them, it turns out, had a grudge against her.

N: And she fights back. Thirteen witnesses come to speak for her. A good Christian, they say, every one of them but one. Why no pork? Elvira says a doctor told her to avoid it, for an illness she was ashamed of.

N: Months pass in her cell. Her baby is born there.

## The room

N: Then the judges vote. The case is not proven. So they order the question, which is what they called torture.

N: She is taken to the chamber. In the room are an inquisitor, a representative of the bishop, a doctor, and a clerk. The clerk has one job. To write down every word she says.

N: Because of him, we can still hear her.

...

N: She is told to tell the truth. She says she has nothing to tell. The cords are tied to her arms, and tightened, turn by turn. And between each turn, she begs. Not for mercy, at first. For instructions.

A: Tell me what you want, for I do not know what to say.

N: Again and again. Tell me what I have to say. She cannot give them what they want, because no one will tell her what it is. That is the rule. The court must never put words in the prisoner's mouth.

...

N: That first day, she confesses nothing. Four days later she is brought back to the room. And before the cords are touched, she breaks. Yes, she says. When I was a girl, my mother told me not to eat pork, and to keep the sabbath. The next day, outside the chamber, she repeats it, as the rules demand.

## The sentence

N: In June of fifteen sixty-eight, she walks in a great public ceremony in Toledo. She is reconciled, as they called it. Taken back into the Church. Her property is confiscated. She must wear a penitent's yellow tunic, and serve three years in prison.

N: After six months the prison term is lifted. She walks out free, and with nothing. The record says she was reduced to begging.

...

N: Her file is still in Spain's national archive in Madrid. Hundreds of pages. A woman who could not read, and whose every cry was written down.

N: Ask yourself what you would have said. Tell me what I have to say.

## Walk on

N: Next, the ceremony she walked in, and the square where Toledo staged the greatest of them. You have stood in it.
