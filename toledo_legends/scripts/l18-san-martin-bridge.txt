# title: Stop 18. The San Martín bridge: the builder's wife
# stop: 18
# room: Puente de San Martín, the medieval bridge over the Tagus on the west side of the old town, below San Juan de los Reyes

## A bridge that must not fall

N: Stop eighteen. The Puente de San Martín crosses the Tagus on the west side of the old town, downhill from San Juan de los Reyes. It is open all night, and lit. Five arches, the middle one huge, and a fortified gate tower at each end. You can walk right across it.

N: The bridge you see was built about six hundred years ago, for an archbishop of Toledo. Its great middle arch was the boldest anyone here had tried. And there is a story about how close it came to failing.

...

A: The builder has worked for years. The great arch is finished, still resting on its wooden frame. Tomorrow the frame comes out, and the city will cross. The archbishop himself will be there.

A: That night, the builder checks his numbers one last time. And he goes white. There is a mistake. When the frame comes away, the arch will not hold. The bridge will fall into the river, in front of the whole city.

N: He tells only one person. His wife. What can she possibly do? Hold that question.

## Fire on the water

A: He lies awake, and at last he sleeps. His wife does not. In the middle of the night she slips out of the house, down to the river, carrying fire. She sets it to the wooden frame under the arch.

A: The dry timber catches. The flames climb into the night. And the unfinished arch cracks, and falls into the Tagus, in the dark, with nobody on it.

...

A: In the morning the city finds a ruin, and blames the fire. Bad luck. Nobody blames the builder. He is given the work again, and this time he builds it right.

A: And his wife? Some say she confessed to the archbishop, and he forgave her, and praised her. Some say a worn stone figure on the bridge is her, still watching over her husband's work.

## What is true

N: This is legend. No record names the builder or his wife, and versions differ. The archbishop who rebuilt the bridge was real, Pedro Tenorio, at the end of the thirteen hundreds.

...

N: Walk to the middle of the bridge and lean over the parapet. Below you is the curve of the great arch. It has held for more than six centuries.

N: She burned what her husband had made, to save him. Would you have dared?

## Walk on

N: Just along the river to the north is the ruined tower of the Bath of La Cava, from stop two. And when the tour ends, play stop eight for the walk home.
