# title: Stop 16. Santo Domingo el Real: Bécquer's three dates
# stop: 16
# room: Plaza de Santo Domingo el Real, a small square below an enclosed convent in the north of the old town

## A window with blue flowers

N: Stop sixteen. Plaza de Santo Domingo el Real is a small, silent square in the north of the old town, below the high blind walls of a convent of enclosed nuns. It is open at any hour. Bécquer never names the square in this story. Stand here tonight, and you will see the kind of place he meant.

...

A: A young artist comes to Toledo to draw. Every day, on his way to the monastery of San Juan de los Reyes, he takes the same narrow, crooked street. And on an old, dark house there is a window. Gothic, with a pot of blue bellflowers, and a thin curtain.

A: One day, as he looks back, the curtain stirs. Someone is watching him. He never sees her face. But for the rest of his stay he dreams of her. Before he leaves Toledo, he writes the date in his sketchbook. The first date.

## A hand in the tower

A: Months later he is back. He wanders into a deserted square. Rubble, weeds, bits of broken pottery shining in the dust. On one side stands a strange building. A Moorish palace once, then a royal house, now a convent, with a bell gable and a latticed tower.

A: He leans against a wall, dreaming. And from the lattice of the tower, a white hand waves. He is alone in the square. It can only be at him.

A: The hand never comes back. He writes down the second date.

...

N: A curtain. A hand. Are they the same woman? Hold that question.

## The third date

A: A year later, on a grey day of snow, he finds himself in the square again. The convent bells are ringing. He goes into the church.

A: A young woman is taking the veil. They cut her hair. They take away her crown of flowers. She lies on the floor as if dead, while the prayers for the dead are sung over her. Then, in the habit, she is led through a door that closes behind her forever.

A: And he knows her face. It is the hand.

A: An old servant tells him who she was. An orphan, whose parents had died of cholera a year before. And she names the street where the girl had lived. His street. The window with the blue flowers.

N: The third date, he says, he wrote down where only he could read it.

## What is true

N: Bécquer tells it as something that happened to him, on his trips to Toledo. Nobody knows if it did. He never named the street, the square or the convent.

...

N: Look up at the convent windows. Did she ever think of him, behind those walls? Bécquer's own answer is the last line of the story. Who knows.

## Walk on

N: Next, down to the old Jewish quarter, to the cellars of a magician. That is stop seventeen.
