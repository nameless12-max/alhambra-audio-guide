# title: Stop 4. The Bitter Well: Raquel and Fernando
# stop: 4
# room: Calle del Pozo Amargo, a small lane in the south of the old town

## A garden with a well

N: Stop four. The Bitter Well gives its name to a short street, Calle del Pozo Amargo, in the south of the old town, towards the river. The well is in a tiny square, open to the street, so you can see it at any hour. Look for a low stone ring, closed over, and a plaque on the wall.

...

N: Picture this lane when it was a garden behind a wall. A well in the middle, with water so sweet the neighbours came for it. And a girl who came to the well every evening.

A: Her name was Raquel. She was the daughter of a Jewish family, the most beautiful girl in Toledo, they say. And one evening, at the garden wall, a young Christian man named Fernando saw her. And she saw him.

A: Nothing could have been more dangerous. A Jewish girl and a Christian boy. In this city, the three faiths lived side by side. But they did not marry.

...

N: So what can two people do, when they cannot be seen together? Hold on to that.

## Meeting in the dark

A: They meet at the well. At night. When the house is asleep, Raquel slips out to the garden, and Fernando climbs the wall. They talk across the stone ring of the well, in whispers. Every night. For months.

A: And then her father finds out. He does not shout. He does not lock her in. He does something worse. He pays a man with a knife.

A: The next night, Fernando does not come. Raquel waits by the well until dawn. And the next night. And the next.

...

A: She never learned what happened. Or maybe she did. She sat by that well and wept, night after night, and her tears fell into the water. And the water turned bitter. From then on, nobody could drink from it.

A: Some say that one night, looking down into the well, she saw his face in the water, and leaned in to reach him. And did not come back up.

## What is true

N: This is a legend, and it changes with every teller. Even the father's name is different from one book to the next. No record shows that Raquel or Fernando ever lived.

N: But the well is real. A medieval well from a Jewish house. Its stone ring was sealed in the nineteen sixties, and you can still see it here. And the street still carries the name of bitter water.

...

N: Put your hand on the stone if you can. Then ask: what would you have done, if you were Raquel, and he never came?

## Walk on

N: Next, out beyond the walls, to a Christ who came down from his cross to keep a promise. That is stop five.
