# title: Stop 6. Bécquer: The Kiss
# stop: 6
# room: Old town lanes near the convent of San Clemente, north-west of the old town

## The poet in the dark streets

N: Stop six. This is a story by a poet, so it has no fixed stop. But the poet does have an address. Gustavo Adolfo Bécquer lived on Calle de San Ildefonso, a quiet street near the convent of San Clemente, in the north-west of the old town. If the tour passes San Clemente, look at the church doorway. Restorers cleaning it recently found a signature in pencil, thought to be his.

...

N: Bécquer was Spain's great romantic poet, a young man from Seville. He loved Toledo at night. He walked these lanes after dark, alone, sketching ruined churches with his brother, the painter Valeriano. And out of those walks came his legends.

## The French captain

A: It is the time of the war against Napoleon. French dragoons ride into Toledo, and there is no room left for them. So they are put up in an old, half-ruined church. Their horses stamp in the chapels.

A: Their captain is young, handsome and bored. And one night, in the dark church, he finds something.

A: On a tomb, by the light of his lamp, kneel two figures carved in stone. A knight in armour. And beside him, his wife. Doña Elvira. Her hands joined in prayer. And she is so beautiful that the captain cannot stop looking at her.

...

N: What happens when a living man falls in love with a woman of stone? Hold on.

## The supper in the church

A: He comes back every night. He tells his fellow officers he has found a lady in Toledo, the loveliest in the city, and they laugh at him. So he invites them to supper. In the church.

A: They light a fire with wood hacked from the carved choir stalls. They drink. They toast the lady. And the captain shows them his lady, kneeling in stone.

A: The captain is drunk now. He mocks the stone knight beside her, the husband who cannot defend her. He raises his glass to him, and throws the wine in his face. And then he says: I will kiss her.

...

A: He climbs up to the tomb. He leans towards that cold stone face. His lips come close.

A: And the officers hear a terrible sound. A cry, and the crash of a body on stone.

A: They run to him. The captain is lying at the foot of the tomb, blood running from his eyes, his nose, his mouth. The stone knight has struck him down, with one terrible blow of his stone gauntlet.

...

## What is true

N: The war was real. French soldiers were quartered in Toledo's convents, and some did terrible damage. But the church in the story, and the lovers on the tomb, are Bécquer's invention.

N: Walk on knowing this: a husband, carved in stone, still keeping watch over his wife. Who would you trust to guard you, after you were gone?

## Walk on

N: Next, a short street, a clockmaker, and a man made of wood. That is stop seven.
