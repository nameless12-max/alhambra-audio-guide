# title: Toledo after dark: the legends
# stop: 0
# room: Anywhere in the old town, before the tour

## A city that keeps its secrets

N: Good evening, Monica. The sun has gone down behind the river, the day crowds have gone home, and Toledo is starting to change.

N: You have seen this city by daylight. Churches, synagogues, a cathedral, the old mosque. Christians, Jews and Muslims, living side by side on one rock above the Tagus for centuries.

N: Tonight is for the other Toledo. The one that people whisper about.

...

N: For hundreds of years, all over Europe, this city's name meant magic. Students were said to come here to learn to raise the dead. Kings were said to lose their kingdoms here by opening the wrong door. Statues here move. Crucifixes speak. A wooden man once walked these streets.

N: Why here? Think about where you are standing. A maze of lanes too narrow for the moon. Three faiths, each with its own scholars and its own secrets. Libraries full of books in languages most of Europe could not read. A city like that breeds stories after dark.

## A Toledan night

N: The Spanish have a phrase for a night when nobody sleeps. Una noche toledana. A Toledan night.

N: One story behind the phrase is the darkest in the city. Listen.

A: More than a thousand years ago, Toledo kept rebelling against the emir in far-off Córdoba. So the emir sent a new governor, a man named Amrus. He announced a great feast to welcome the prince, and invited the leading men of Toledo to his palace. They came in, one by one, through a single gate. And one by one, as they entered, they were killed. Their bodies went into a pit dug in secret. Hundreds of them. Toledo did not sleep that night. And it did not rebel again for a long time.

...

N: Arab chroniclers tell this massacre as history, and some scholars doubt it. And the phrase may come from something smaller. An old dictionary from four centuries ago blamed it on the mosquitoes, which ate visitors alive in the summer heat.

## How to use these tracks

N: At seven, you join the legends tour. It will be in Spanish.

N: Each track here tells you one story in English, before you get there. Listen over a drink, or standing at the place itself. Then, when the guide stops, drops their voice and points, you will know exactly what they are pointing at. You will know when the horse kneels, and when the statue moves.

N: One promise. When I tell you a legend, you will hear it in another voice. When I tell you what really happened, it will be me. Keep them apart, and enjoy both.

...

N: Some of the places are shut at night, and you will only see their doors and walls. That is how legends should be met. From outside, in the dark.

## Walk on

N: Start with the little mosque by the old gate, and a horse that would not take another step. That is stop one.
