# title: Stop 10. Galiana: the princess and the prince from France
# stop: 10
# room: Museo de Santa Cruz and the Miradero viewpoint, just below Zocodover through the Arco de la Sangre

## A garden above the river

N: Stop ten. Walk from Zocodover through the Arco de la Sangre and down the slope. Here is the Museo de Santa Cruz, an old hospital with a great carved stone doorway, lit at night. A little further on is the Miradero, a terrace that looks north over the dark plain.

N: Toledans say the palaces of a princess stood right here, in the old fortress quarter that ran from the arch to this viewpoint.

...

A: Long ago, a Moorish king ruled Toledo. Galafre. He had one daughter, Galiana, the most beautiful woman in Spain. She lived among gardens and fountains, with everything a princess could want. Except love.

A: A suitor came often from Guadalajara, a powerful lord. In the old French poem he is a giant called Bramante. Toledans call him Abenzaide. He was proud and cruel, and she could not bear him. One version says he loved her so much that he dug a tunnel all the way from his city to hers, to see her every day.

N: And at her father's court there was a guest nobody paid much attention to. Hold on to him.

## The stranger in the garden

A: He is a young prince of the Franks, far from home, sheltering with the Moorish king. One summer night, in the palace garden, Galiana tells her maid a secret. She does not love Abenzaide. And the young stranger, hidden among the trees, hears every word.

A: He steps out. He tells her he loves her. And she finds that she loves him.

...

A: The rejected suitor swears revenge. The king's astrologers advise a duel to the death. The whole city comes to watch. And the young prince drives his sword into his rival's heart.

A: Galiana leaves with him for France. She is baptised. They marry. And the young prince's name? Charles. One day the world will call him Charlemagne.

## What is true

N: Charlemagne never lived in Toledo. The story comes from a French poem of the Middle Ages about his youth, and versions differ. In some, she becomes his queen. In others, he steals away with her by night, and her father's horsemen ride after them.

N: Across the river, in the gardens of the plain, there is a real building called the Palace of Galiana. It was built for a Muslim king of Toledo in the eleventh century, long after Charlemagne, and it only took her name about five hundred years ago.

...

N: Look out from the Miradero into the dark. Somewhere out there is the palace that borrowed her name. Up here, the story says, is the garden where she heard a stranger speak. Would you have stepped out from the trees?

## Walk on

N: Next, up behind the Alcázar, to the knights of the Temple and a font that filled with blood. That is stop eleven.
