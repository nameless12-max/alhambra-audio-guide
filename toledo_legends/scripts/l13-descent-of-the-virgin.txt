# title: Stop 13. The cathedral door: the night the Virgin came down
# stop: 13
# room: Plaza del Ayuntamiento, the town-hall square, in front of the cathedral's main west front

## A bishop on his way to prayer

N: Stop thirteen. Stand in the Plaza del Ayuntamiento, the town-hall square, facing the great west front of the cathedral. It is lit at night. Look at the tallest of its doors, in the middle: the Door of Forgiveness, the Puerta del Perdón. Above it, carved in stone, is tonight's story.

...

N: Long before this cathedral, an older church stood here. Its bishop, thirteen centuries ago, was Ildefonso, a scholar who had written a book in praise of the Virgin Mary. He is Toledo's own saint.

A: It is the middle of the night. Ildefonso and his clergy walk to the church for the night prayers. As they come near the doors, they see light inside. A blaze of light, brighter than any candle. The others fall back in fear. Ildefonso goes in alone.

A: And there, in his own bishop's chair, sits the Virgin.

...

A: She calls him to her. And she gives him a gift from heaven. A priest's vestment, a chasuble, to wear in her honour.

N: Now look above the door. That is the scene. The Virgin, seated, handing the vestment to a kneeling bishop.

## The stone and the veil

A: Where her feet touched the ground, they say, the stone became holy. That stone is still inside the cathedral, in a small chapel called the Chapel of the Descent, behind a strong grille.

A: And Toledo has a second wonder for Ildefonso. On the feast of Leocadia, the city's young martyr, the bishop was praying at her tomb before the king and his court. And the great stone lid lifted by itself. Leocadia rose, and spoke to him, praising his book.

A: Ildefonso wanted proof. He reached for her veil and called for a blade, and nobody could hear him in the uproar. Then the king himself held out his own knife. The bishop cut a piece of the veil. And the saint went back into the dark.

N: Among the cathedral's relics there is still a piece of veil, kept with a knife.

## What is true

N: These are legends. Ildefonso was real, a bishop of Toledo in the six hundreds, and his book on the Virgin is real. The first written account of the vision comes about eighty years after his death. One historian thinks the veil was simply a relic he once cut and kept, and that the miracle grew around it.

N: But Toledo has never let the story go. His feast, every January, is one of the great days of the city.

...

N: Look up at the carving one more time. A man who spent his life writing about someone he had never seen. And one night, the story says, she came.

## Walk on

N: Next, round the side of the cathedral, a thief locked inside it after dark. That is stop fourteen.
