# title: Stop 14. The cathedral at night: Bécquer's golden bracelet
# stop: 14
# room: Outside the cathedral, along its north side under the bell tower, on Calle Cardenal Cisneros

## A woman in tears

N: Stop fourteen. Walk from the town-hall square along the north side of the cathedral, under the great bell tower. At night the doors are shut and the walls climb into the dark. Somewhere behind them, in her own chapel, is the Virgin of the Sagrario, the patroness of Toledo.

N: This is Bécquer again, the poet of stop six. And it is his most frightening tale.

...

A: Down by the Tagus, a young man finds the woman he loves in tears. María Antúnez. Beautiful, spoilt, used to having whatever she wants. Pedro Alfonso de Orellana is brave, and superstitious, and he would do anything for her.

A: At the great feast of the Virgin, she tells him, she saw a bracelet. A golden band set with diamonds, on the arm of the Virgin of the Sagrario. And since that day she can think of nothing else.

N: He tells her it is impossible. Then he goes away and thinks about it. What would you do for someone you love? Hold that question.

## Alone in the dark

A: Pedro hides in the cathedral as the doors are locked. Night falls. He creeps towards the high altar. He feels the tombstones under his feet holding him back, as if the dead were gripping his ankles. He goes on.

A: He climbs the altar. He reaches the image. He tears the bracelet from the Virgin's arm.

A: Then he opens his eyes.

...

A: The cathedral is full. Every statue has stepped down from its tomb and its niche. Saints and nuns, angels and demons, warriors and peasants, crowding the aisles. Marble archbishops kneel. Stone monsters crawl over the pillars and the vaults. And every one of them is looking at him.

A: He screams. And falls.

A: In the morning they find him at the foot of the altar, the bracelet still in his fist. He is laughing and crying at once. Hers, he says. Hers. Pedro had gone mad.

## What is true

N: The thief and his lady are Bécquer's invention. The Virgin of the Sagrario is real, and so are her jewels. Bécquer never tells us whether the statues really moved, or whether guilt did it all.

## The lady with the candles

A: One more ghost for these walls. On foggy nights, they say, a woman in white walks around the cathedral, near its cloister doors, carrying a silver candlestick. Wind and rain never touch her flames. Some say she was betrayed in love and is still searching for him. Others say she guards something buried beneath the cathedral.

N: That one is a modern tale, with no old book behind it. Look along the walls anyway.

...

N: Look up at the tower. And remember Pedro, who got exactly what he went in for, and lost his mind.

## Walk on

N: Next, west across the old town, to a table made for King Solomon. That is stop fifteen.
