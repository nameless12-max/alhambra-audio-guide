# title: Stop 7. Street of the Wooden Man: Juanelo's automaton
# stop: 7
# room: Calle del Hombre de Palo, a short street near the cathedral

## A man who was not a man

N: Stop seven. The Calle del Hombre de Palo, the Street of the Wooden Man, is a short lane of about a hundred metres near the cathedral. It is open at all hours. Look for the street sign, and for a plaque that remembers who once walked here.

...

N: Picture this lane four and a half centuries ago. Morning. Shopkeepers opening their shutters. And down the middle of the street, stiff and slow, walks a man made of wood.

A: He bows to the people he passes. He carries a bowl. At the archbishop's palace, they say, he stops, and someone fills the bowl with food.

N: Who made him? And why would a machine beg for bread? Hold on to that.

## The emperor's clockmaker

N: His maker was real. Juanelo Turriano, an Italian clockmaker, the greatest of his age. He built marvellous clocks for the emperor Charles the Fifth, and stayed in Spain to serve his son.

N: And here in Toledo he did something nobody thought possible. The city sits high on its rock, and the river runs far below. Water had to be carried up by donkey. Juanelo built a vast machine of wood and metal that lifted the water of the Tagus all the way up to the Alcázar, the fortress at the top of the hill. It was finished in fifteen sixty-nine.

...

N: But here is the turn. He was owed money for it, and he was not paid in full. The great engineer grew old and poor.

A: And so, the legend says, he built one last machine. A wooden servant, to walk out each day and fetch what his master could no longer pay for. The people of Toledo grew fond of it. They dropped coins in its bowl. And they named the street after it.

...

## What is true

N: Juanelo, his water machine, and his money troubles are history. He died in Toledo in fifteen eighty-five. The walking wooden man is legend. Some writers think there was only a wooden figure standing still, collecting alms. Others believe he really did build moving figures, which would not be surprising for a man who made clocks that showed the heavens.

N: Even then, Toledans whispered that a man who could make water climb a mountain must be a magician.

## The devil's alley

A: One more street, if the tour takes you near Zocodover, the main square. There is a narrow, dark alley there called the Alley of the Devil. They say it is named for old stories of witchcraft and pacts with the devil. And on windy nights, they say, you can still hear whispering between the walls.

N: That one, nobody has ever proved. Listen anyway.

...

N: Think of the old man. A genius, forgotten. And his last invention is a servant who never leaves him. What would you build, if you could not ask anyone for help?

## Walk on

N: There are more stories waiting on the map. Next is Zocodover, and its fires. That is stop nine.
