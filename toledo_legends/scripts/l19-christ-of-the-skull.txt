# title: Stop 19. The Christ of the Skull: a walk home, and goodnight
# stop: 19
# room: Calle del Cristo de la Calavera, a short lane east of the cathedral

## A glove on the floor

N: Stop nineteen, the last one. Listen to this on the way back, in any dark lane. The story belongs to a short street east of the cathedral, still called the street of the Christ of the Skull. A small Christ once hung there under an archway, lit by a little lamp, with a skull at his feet.

...

N: It is Bécquer again. And it begins with a party.

A: The king is about to ride to war against the Moors. On the last night, there is a great feast at the Alcázar. And the most beautiful woman at court, Doña Inés, drops her glove. A perfumed glove, with gold buttons.

A: Two knights reach for it at the same moment. Two old friends, born in Toledo, who learned to fight side by side. Now they are rivals for the same woman. The king has to step between them.

...

A: So they agree to settle it that night. With swords. They walk through the sleeping city to the only light they can find. The little lamp in front of the Christ of the Skull.

## The lamp

A: They draw. And the moment their blades touch, the lamp goes out. Total darkness.

A: They step apart. And the lamp lights again, by itself.

A: They try again. Steel touches steel. Darkness. They part. Light.

...

A: And then a voice. From somewhere in the dark lane. Low, strange. They cannot understand a single word. But both men freeze, and their swords fall from their hands.

N: What does a man do, when heaven itself refuses to light his duel? Hold on.

## The balcony

A: They make peace. And together they walk to her house. They are below her balcony as the sky turns grey.

A: And at dawn, a rope comes down from that balcony. And down the rope climbs a man. A third man. And Doña Inés leans out to wave him goodbye.

...

A: The two knights look at each other. And they burst out laughing. Friends again. The next day, as the army rides out, Doña Inés sees them laughing at her. And one tear of spite rolls down her face.

N: That is a legend, by a poet. Nobody fought here. But Toledans still tell it, and it is a good one to carry home through dark streets.

## Goodnight

N: So, Monica. Tonight you have heard a horse kneel, a king break the locks, a well turn bitter, a crucifix speak, and a stone knight strike. None of it is history. All of it is Toledo.

N: If the Spanish tour carried you past these doors tonight, I hope you knew exactly where you were.

...

N: Tomorrow, the tracks take you back to these places by daylight, with the real stories behind the walls.

N: Walk slowly. Watch the lamps. And if one of them goes out as you pass, keep walking. Goodnight.
