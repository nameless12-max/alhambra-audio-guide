# title: Stop 17. The House of Villena: the magician in the flask
# stop: 17
# room: Museo del Greco, Paseo del Tránsito, in the old Jewish quarter in the south-west of the old town

## The cellars of the magician

N: Stop seventeen. The El Greco Museum stands on the Paseo del Tránsito, in the old Jewish quarter, next to the Synagogue of El Tránsito. At night it is closed. You will see garden walls and an old house of brick and timber, rebuilt a century ago.

N: Before it was El Greco's museum, Toledans called the houses on this spot the Houses of Villena. And they said that under them, in the cellars, a magician worked.

...

A: Enrique de Villena. A nobleman of royal blood, a scholar who read the stars and wrote about everything. And, all Spain believed, a necromancer. A master of the dark arts.

A: They say he learned them from the devil himself, in a cave at Salamanca. And that when the devil came to claim him, Villena slipped away, and left the devil holding only his shadow. For the rest of his life, they say, he cast none.

N: A man who can cheat the devil once will want to cheat death too. Hold on to that.

## The flask

A: He has read the day of his death in the stars. So he gives his most loyal servant a terrible order. When I die, cut my body into pieces. Put them in a great glass flask, in the liquid I have prepared. Seal it, and wait. Slowly the pieces will join. And I will live again, forever.

A: The servant obeys. The flask is hidden in a crypt in Toledo. Months pass.

...

A: Then two knights come upon it in the dark. As they lean close to the glass, something inside moves. A shapeless mass of flesh and bone lunges at them. They cut it down with their swords. And the floor of the crypt cracks open, and a chariot of fire rises, and carries his soul away to hell.

## What is true

N: Villena was real. He died of a fever, in Madrid, in fourteen thirty-four. After his death, a bishop had many of his books burned. That is how a scholar became a sorcerer. The flask is a popular tale, and versions differ.

N: The cellars are real too. Tradition says a rich treasurer of the king, Samuel ha-Leví, who built the synagogue next door, hid his gold in them. Nobody has found it.

N: And the painter? El Greco rented rooms in these houses when they were already half in ruins. Centuries later, romantic writers decided he too had been mad. Others blamed his eyes. Neither is true. His figures are long because he chose to paint them that way.

...

N: Stand by the wall and think of the servant, alone, with the knife and the flask. What would you have done, if your master asked that of you?

## Walk on

N: Next, down to the river, to a bridge, and a woman who set it on fire. That is stop eighteen.
