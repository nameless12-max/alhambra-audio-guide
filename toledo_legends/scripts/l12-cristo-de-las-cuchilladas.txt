# title: Stop 12. Plaza de San Justo: the Christ of the Knife Cuts
# stop: 12
# room: Plaza de San Justo, a small square south of the Alcázar, between the cathedral and San Miguel el Alto

## The Christ on the wall

N: Stop twelve. Plaza de San Justo is a small, sloping square a few minutes south of the Alcázar, in front of the church of the Saints Justo and Pastor. It is open at any hour. Look at the old stone wall of the church, and for a plaque in the square that tells this story.

...

N: Picture this square more than five hundred years ago. Toledo is at war with itself. Two great families, the Silvas and the Ayalas, fight for the city in its streets. Swords come out at the slightest insult.

A: Diego de Ayala is in love with Isabel, a daughter of the enemy house. Their love is a secret. Every evening, on his way to her, Diego stops here to pray before a Christ on the church wall. The Christ of Mercy.

A: One night, as he prays, he hears screams. Masked men are crossing the square, dragging a woman with her hands tied. Isabel. Her own family has come to take her away from him.

N: One man against her whole family. Hold on.

## Ten swords against one

A: Diego draws his sword. He fights alone, against ten. He wounds one of them. But they drive him back, bleeding, until he is pressed against the church wall, under the very Christ he had been praying to.

A: He cannot win. So he asks for one thing only. Save her.

...

A: And the wall opens. The stones part behind them, draw Diego and Isabel inside the church, and close again.

A: The attackers hack at the wall in fury. A hundred blows of steel on stone. The wall holds. They throw themselves at the church doors. The doors hold too. And then the bells begin to ring, louder and louder, until the whole neighbourhood comes running into the square, and the masked men flee into the dark.

A: From that night, the Christ of Mercy had a new name. The Christ of the Knife Cuts. El Cristo de las Cuchilladas.

## What is true

N: This is legend, and versions differ. In some, the wall swallows the lovers. In others, it only turns every sword away. Nobody knows what became of them afterwards.

N: But the feud was real. In those years Toledo's great families fought for control of the city, sometimes in lanes like this one.

...

N: Run your eyes along the old stones in the torchlight. Every scar in them, the tellers say, is a sword that could not reach her. Who would you ask, if you had your back against a wall?

## Walk on

N: Next, to the cathedral, where the queen of heaven came down one night. That is stop thirteen.
