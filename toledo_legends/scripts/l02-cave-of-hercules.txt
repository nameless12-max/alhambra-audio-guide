# title: Stop 2. The Cave of Hercules: the king who broke the locks
# stop: 2
# room: Cueva de Hércules, Callejón de San Ginés, a few minutes from the cathedral

## The house of many locks

N: Stop two. The Cave of Hercules is under a narrow alley called Callejón de San Ginés, a short walk from the cathedral. It opens only for a few hours in the daytime, so tonight you will stand at a plain door in the alley and look down, in your mind, into the dark.

...

A: Long ago, they say, the hero Hercules came to Spain and founded this city. He was strong, but he was also a magician. And under this hill he built an enchanted palace. Inside, he left a secret. Then he sealed the door with a lock.

A: Every king who ruled in Toledo after him came here and added one more lock. Century after century, the door grew heavier with iron.

...

N: Until a king came who wanted to know. What could be worth so many locks?

## Rodrigo opens the door

A: His name was Rodrigo, the last king of the Goths. He believed there was treasure inside. His lords begged him not to. He broke the locks anyway, one after another, and walked in with a torch.

A: There was no gold. There was only a chest. And in the chest, a folded cloth. He unfolded it. Painted on it were horsemen he had never seen. Strange faces. Turbans on their heads. Bows and lances in their hands. And words beneath them. Whoever opens this, men like these will take his kingdom.

...

N: Imagine him holding that cloth. The torch shaking. Every lock on the floor around him, broken by his own hand.

A: Within a year, the riders came. They crossed the sea from Africa, and Rodrigo rode out to meet them, and he was never seen again. The Goths' Spain was gone.

## The girl by the river

A: And why did they come? Toledo has a second story for that. Go down to the river, by the old bridge of San Martín, and you will find a ruined tower called the Bath of La Cava. They say that from it, King Rodrigo watched a young girl bathing in the Tagus. Florinda, the daughter of Count Julian. He wanted her, and he took her. Her father, who guarded the strait for the king, swore revenge. And he opened the way across the sea for the invaders.

...

N: Now the history. Rodrigo was real. He really was the last Gothic king, and he really did lose his kingdom in battle in seven hundred and eleven. The locked palace, the painted riders and Florinda are legend, written down by chroniclers long after.

N: And the cave itself? Archaeologists say it is a Roman water tank, part of the system that fed the city. The tower by the river is the stump of a medieval bridge.

...

N: Stand at the door and ask yourself the king's question. Would you have opened it?

## Walk on

N: Next, the city of magicians, and the man who could make you live a whole life in an afternoon. That is stop three.
