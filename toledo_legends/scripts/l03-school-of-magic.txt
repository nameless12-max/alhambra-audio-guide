# title: Stop 3. Toledo, school of magic: Don Illán and the Dean
# stop: 3
# room: Old town lanes; the guide may stop near the cathedral or a cellar

## The city where they taught the dark arts

N: Stop three. This story has no single address. The tour may tell it in a lane near the cathedral, or at the mouth of a cellar.

...

N: In the Middle Ages, across Europe, if a man said he had studied in Toledo, people crossed themselves. The Toledan arts meant necromancy. Students were said to learn it in caves beneath this city.

N: Here is the real root of the rumour. After the Christian conquest, scholars came here from all over Europe. Toledo had what no one else had: Arabic books on stars, medicine, mathematics, the Greek philosophers. Christians, Jews and Muslims sat together and turned those books into Latin. Astronomy looked like sorcery to people who could not read it. So Toledo became the city of magicians.

## The Dean and the magician

N: A Spanish prince wrote the most famous of these stories seven centuries ago. Listen.

A: A churchman, the Dean of Santiago, rides all the way to Toledo. He has heard of a master of magic, Don Illán. He begs to be taught. Don Illán looks at him and says: men like you rise high, and then forget who helped them. The Dean swears he will never forget.

A: So Don Illán agrees. And he calls to his housekeeper. Roast us two partridges for supper. But do not cook them until I tell you.

...

A: Then, the lesson begins. Messengers arrive. The Dean's uncle, the archbishop, is dead. The Dean is made bishop. Then archbishop. Then cardinal, in Rome. Then Pope. Years pass. And at every step, Don Illán asks one small favour. A post for his son. And every time, the Dean says: not yet. Next time.

A: At last, the Pope loses patience. He calls his old teacher a heretic and a sorcerer, and threatens him with prison. He will not even give him food for the road home.

...

A: Don Illán says quietly: then I will eat the partridges I ordered tonight. And he calls out to his housekeeper.

A: And the Dean is sitting in a cellar in Toledo. The same evening. The same chair. No Rome. No crown. The partridges are still raw. It was all a spell, to see who he really was.

N: He was ashamed, says the book, and did not know what to say. Don Illán sent him home without his lesson.

## The table of Solomon

A: One more treasure belongs to this magic city. They say the conquerors of Toledo found here the table of King Solomon, made of gold and jewels, and on it the secret name of God. Two generals fought over it. One broke off a leg to prove he found it first.

N: That is legend too. Arab chroniclers wrote it down, and the table has never been found.

...

N: Every one of us has made the Dean's promise to someone. Would you have kept it?
