# title: Stop 1. Cristo de la Luz: the horse that knelt
# stop: 1
# room: Mosque of Cristo de la Luz, near the Puerta del Sol gate, north side of the old town

## The horse that would not move

N: Stop one. The small brick mosque of Cristo de la Luz stands on a steep lane just inside the old walls, near the Puerta del Sol gate. It closes before dark, so tonight you will see it from outside: a little square building of red brick, with arches over arches in its walls.

...

N: It is older than almost anything around you. An inscription on its front gives the year it was finished, nine hundred and ninety-nine. A thousand years ago, this was a neighbourhood mosque.

N: Now the story. Picture a spring day, almost a century later. Toledo has opened its gates to a Christian king.

A: King Alfonso rides in at the head of his army, in triumph, up this very slope. Beside him rides his greatest knight, they say, the Cid. And then, here, outside this little mosque, the king's horse stops.

A: It will not go on. The king spurs it. Nothing. And then, in front of the whole army, the horse slowly bends its front legs, and kneels in the street. Facing the wall of the mosque.

...

N: Why would a horse kneel to a wall? Hold that question.

## The light in the wall

A: The king orders the wall opened. The soldiers strike at the stones. And behind them they find a hollow, a hidden niche. Inside it, a crucifix. And beside the crucifix, an oil lamp. Still burning.

A: Here is the secret, as the legend tells it. Nearly four hundred years before, when the Muslim armies came, the Christians of Toledo hid their Christ in this wall to save it, and lit a lamp beside it. They sealed it up. They died. Nobody remembered. And all that time, in the dark, the little flame kept burning. Waiting for someone to come back for it.

...

N: That is why the building is called Christ of the Light. And they say the king held the first Christian Mass here, and left his shield behind, with a cross on it, hanging in the church.

N: Now the history. The king was real, and he did take Toledo in ten eighty-five. The mosque really did become a chapel. But the legend appears only after the conquest, told by the winners. Some tellings even forget the lamp. Others put the Cid on the horse. The kneeling horse belongs to the story, not the record.

## The stone in the street

N: When you are outside, look down at the paving in front of the building. Toledans say one white stone marks the spot where the horse went down on its knees. Look for it with the guide's torch.

...

N: And stand a moment with this. The lamp of the legend burned for a faith that was hidden. The building that kept it was built by another faith. Whose light is it?

## Walk on

N: Next, down under the streets near the cathedral, to a palace no king was ever meant to open. That is stop two.
