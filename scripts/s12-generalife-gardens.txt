# title: Stop 12. The gardens and the water
# stop: 12

## Where you are

N: Stop twelve. The gardens and the water.

N: This is the Generalife, the sultans' country estate on the hill facing the Alhambra. It has its own signed, one-way route. Follow it, and take the next stops in whatever order it gives you.

N: You are in the new gardens below the palace. Around you are hedged walks, walls of clipped cypress and long pools, and an open-air theatre. Now look ahead and up the slope. The building above you is the summer palace.

## The New Gardens

...

N: These gardens are not medieval. They are the Jardines Nuevos, the New Gardens, laid out between nineteen thirty-one and nineteen fifty-one by the restorers Torres Balbás and Prieto-Moreno. The theatre followed in nineteen fifty-two.

N: Now look down the slope. The terraced orchards there have been farmed since the thirteen hundreds.

## A country estate

...

N: The Generalife was never only a garden. It was an almunia, a country estate that was both a retreat and a working farm, a short walk from the palace.

N: It probably began under Muhammad the Second, at the end of the twelve hundreds. An inscription records that Isma'il the First remodelled it in thirteen nineteen. Yusuf the Third reworked the southern parts in the fourteen hundreds. Another inscription, by Ibn al-Jayyab, calls the estate the House of the Felicitous Kingdom.

## How the water arrives

...

N: Now listen. Wherever you stand on this estate, you can hear water moving. All of it arrives by gravity. Here is how, in three steps.

N: First, the source. Far up the valley, about six kilometres upstream, a channel takes water from the river Darro. That channel is the Acequia Real, the royal channel. It runs gently downhill along the side of the valley, all the way here.

N: Second, the split. Several kilometres before the estate, a smaller channel branches off. This is the Acequia del Tercio. It runs along higher ground, to feed the upper gardens and the Water Stairway. The main channel stays lower, and runs right through the palace above you.

N: Third, the join. Past a medieval reservoir called the Albercones, the two channels meet again. Together they go on to the Alhambra. They enter it by the Water Tower, the Torre del Agua, and fill its pools and fountains.

N: So, one river. A high channel and a low channel. One slow slope across a whole valley. That is the engine behind every pool and fountain you have seen on this hill.

...

N: Now look up, to the hilltop above the estate. The ruined fort up there is the Silla del Moro. It was a lookout, and one of its jobs was to guard this water supply.

## Stories you will hear

...

A: Often told, thinly sourced. A recent documentary describes very deep wells on the hill above the Generalife. In them, it says, waterwheels turned by animals lifted water further uphill, in stages. Treat the details as approximate.

A: The same programme claims the Nasrids had a pressure device that pushed water up to the Alcazaba, and that nobody else in medieval Iberia could do it. We found nothing to support this. Treat it with real caution.

A: And a myth check on the name. You will hear that Generalife comes from the Arabic Jannat al-'Arif, the Garden of the Architect. Scholars dispute this, and other readings have been proposed.

## Walk on

...

N: When you are ready, follow the signed route up through the gardens to the palace entrance. Beyond it lies a long courtyard with water running down the middle. That is stop thirteen.
