# title: Stop 20. Out by the Puerta de la Justicia
# stop: 20

N: Stop twenty. Out by the Puerta de la Justicia.

N: You are inside the walls, in front of the great gate in the southern wall. This is the Puerta de la Justicia, the Gate of Justice. If it is open as an exit, it is your way out. Do not hurry through it. Stop first, facing the inner arch.

...

N: Look up, above the inner arch. A key is carved there. You saw the same key earlier, on the Wine Gate.

...

N: Now look above the key. In a niche stands a Gothic Virgin and Child. This one is a copy. The original was carved in fifteen oh one and is attributed to Roberto Alemán. It is the statue you heard about in the museum, upstairs in the Palace of Charles the Fifth.

N: Now find the Arabic writing. This is the foundation inscription. It dates the gate to thirteen forty-eight, under Yusuf the First. Stones that record a building's founding like this are rare in the Alhambra.

N: Look also for an inscription inlaid in white marble from Macael, set with slate. It is the only documented use of that technique anywhere in the Alhambra.

...

N: Think about what you are looking at. The Catholic Monarchs set their Virgin over a sultan's foundation inscription. It is the layering of conquest you have seen all day, in one small space.

N: The gate's Arabic name is Bab al-Sharia. It is usually translated as the Gate of the Esplanade, and sometimes as the Gate of the Law.

...

N: Now walk slowly through the passage. Count the arches as you pass under them. Look at the great wooden door. It is the original, sheathed in metal.

...

A: Often told, and only partly confirmed. The passage is usually described as a bent entrance. It turns at right angles, so that attackers could not charge straight through. Watch for the turns as you walk. We could not confirm how many there are.

...

N: When you are outside, turn around and face the gate.

...

N: Look up, to the stone at the very top of the great outer arch, the keystone. An open hand is carved on it.

...

A: A myth check on the hand and the key. Both are Islamic symbols, and their exact meaning is debated. The hand is popularly read as the five pillars of Islam, or as a charm against the evil eye.

A: You will hear that judges once held court under this gate. There is no record to support it.

A: And there is the legend Washington Irving retells. The Alhambra will stand until the hand reaches down and grasps the key. On that day, the whole fortress will fall. The hand is out here, and the key is on the inner arch. They have not met yet.

A: One last thing you will hear. The fountain of Charles the Fifth, beside the gate, is said to carry the arms of Burgundy and Lorraine among its imperial heraldry. That too is thinly sourced.

...

N: From here the path drops through the woods to the Cuesta de Gomérez, and down to the Plaza Nueva in the city.

N: If you need the Access Pavilion, for a bus or a car, go back through the gate instead. Walk east along the Calle Real and the Cypress Walk. It takes about twenty minutes.

N: Before you go, there is one last track, the closing. Play it here, or on the way down the hill.
