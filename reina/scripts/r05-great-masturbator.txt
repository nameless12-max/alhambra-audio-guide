# title: Stop 5. Dalí: The Great Masturbator
# stop: 5
# room: 205.13

## A summer visit

N: Stop five. You are in room two hundred and five point thirteen, on the second floor. Look for a wide canvas with a huge soft pale head, slumped on the ground, its nose pressed into the earth, an insect clinging to its face.

...

N: Go back to the bay you saw at stop three. The same summer house. A few years later.

N: Dalí is now twenty-five, and suddenly famous. He and his friend from student days, Luis Buñuel, have just made a short film in Paris. It opens with a razor slicing an eye. Audiences gasp. The Surrealists, the wildest artists in Paris, want to know these two Spaniards.

N: And that summer, some of them come to visit Dalí at the bay. Among them is a poet, Paul Éluard, and his wife.

## Gala

...

N: Her name is Gala. She is Russian. Ten years older than Dalí. Sharp, hungry, utterly sure of herself. Dalí is shy and nervous.

N: The holiday ends. Her husband goes back to Paris. Gala does not. She stays behind, with Dalí.

N: In the weeks that follow, he paints this picture. So what is it? What happened to him that summer?

...

## The head

N: Look at the great pale head first. Find the closed eye, and the long eyelashes, resting on the ground. Experts read this head as Dalí himself, melting, overwhelmed, turned inside out by what is happening to him.

...

N: Now find the grasshopper, fixed to the face. As a boy, Dalí was so afraid of grasshoppers that his schoolmates would throw them at him to watch him panic. Here it clings to his own mouth.

A: That schoolyard detail is the story Dalí liked to tell. Like most of his stories, it may be a little improved.

...

N: Now look for the ants, crawling near the mouth. To Dalí, ants meant decay. And now look for a woman's face, rising out of the head, pressed close to a man's body.

N: Desire and terror, in the same painting. A young man who wants something desperately, and is frightened half to death that he will get it. Does that feel strange to look at? It is meant to. Stay with it a moment.

...

## The price

N: He got her. Gala never left. She became his wife, his muse, his manager, for the rest of her life.

N: But it cost him. His father saw a married older woman and a ruined son, and threw him out of the family house. You remember the sister at the window. She took her father's side.

N: The painting was still his when he died. He left it to the Spanish state. It is one of the treasures of this museum, and it will never be sold.

## An elephant for an ashtray

...

N: One more story, for the road. Nearly forty years after this summer, Air India asked Dalí to design a gift for its passengers. He made a porcelain ashtray, a swan that turns into an elephant. As his fee, he asked for a baby elephant. And the airline sent one. A young elephant was flown from India to Europe, and delivered to the painter.

## Three friends

...

N: Remember the friends of his student days. Buñuel the filmmaker. Lorca the poet.

A: They say Lorca was hurt by that famous film. Its title was An Andalusian Dog, and Lorca was an Andalusian. Buñuel later hinted that the joke was on him. Maybe it was. Lorca certainly felt left behind.

N: Seven summers after this painting, the war began. In its first weeks, Lorca was taken outside Granada and shot. His body has never been found.

...

N: Look one last time at the head on the ground, its eye closed. That summer he got what he wanted. In the years that followed, he lost his family, and then his friend.

## Walk on

N: That war leads to the painting at the heart of this museum. Follow the signs for Guernica, to room two hundred and five point ten, on this floor. Every guard knows the way. That is stop six.
