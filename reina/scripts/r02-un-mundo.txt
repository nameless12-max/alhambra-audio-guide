# title: Stop 2. Ángeles Santos: A World
# stop: 2
# room: 205.06

## A girl in a quiet city

N: Stop two. You are in room two hundred and five point zero six, on the second floor. Look for the huge, almost square canvas, three metres each way, a tilted world of houses, stairs and stars.

...

N: Picture a provincial city in the middle of Spain. Grey streets. Church bells. Shutters closed against the heat. In one of those houses lives a teenage girl. Her father is a customs official. She has hardly been anywhere.

N: Her name is Ángeles Santos. And she has decided to paint the entire world.

## The book

...

N: How does a girl in a quiet city find the new art of Europe? Through a book. She gets hold of a German critic's survey of the latest painting, full of pictures she has never seen. Strange, sharp, dreamlike. She studies it. Then she starts a canvas far taller than she is.

N: Stand in front of it now and ask yourself one question. What kind of mind does this, at that age?

...

N: Look at the centre. The world is not round. It is a cube, floating in space, and the houses cling to it at mad angles, like they might slide off.

...

N: Now find the staircase. It climbs from the world up into the sky. Follow it with your eye. Who is on it?

...

N: Women. Look. Some carry stars up the stairs. Some light the stars with fire, like lamplighters. Find them. In this world, it is the women who keep the universe burning.

N: Now look down into the houses. Find the figures in rooms, the mothers, the musicians, the long strange hair. Everyone busy, everyone separate. A whole universe, and somehow everyone in it is alone.

## The revelation

...

N: She sends it to the Autumn Salon in Madrid. Nobody knows her name. The canvas is enormous. People stop dead.

N: The most famous showman of Madrid's literary world travels out to find her. He writes that he has found a revelation, and that the revelation is a girl of seventeen. Poets come to her door. For a few months, the quiet city has a genius, and she is a teenager.

N: Can you imagine it? At seventeen, the whole of literary Spain knocking on your door.

## The cost

...

N: Here the story darkens. Her painting turns wilder, rougher, angrier. Something is pulling at her.

A: The story usually told is this. Soon after her triumph, she ran away from home. Her family found her, and her father had her shut away in a sanatorium. Accounts differ on the details, and it is hard to pin down. But it is told so often that it has become part of her.

...

N: When she painted again, the visions were gone. She married, she painted gentle, ordinary pictures, and for decades the world forgot that she had ever made this. The girl who painted the universe became a quiet painter of the everyday.

N: And yet she lived to be over a hundred. She lived long enough to see A World rediscovered, and to see crowds stand where you are standing now.

## The stairs

...

N: Look one more time at the women on the stairs, carrying their stars into the dark. A girl of seventeen painted that. And then she spent the rest of her life on the ground.

...

N: What would she have painted next, if they had let her?

## Walk on

N: Stay in this room. Find a smaller picture of a young woman seen from behind, at a window, looking out to sea. That is stop three.
