# title: Stop 1. Picasso: Woman in Blue
# stop: 1
# room: 201.02

## The boy with no money

N: Stop one. You are on the second floor of the Sabatini building, room two hundred and one point zero two. Look for a tall canvas of a woman in a long blue dress and a huge feathered hat, her face powdered white.

...

N: Before you look closely, picture a cold room in Madrid. A young man of nineteen sits in his coat, because there is no money for heat. He has already tasted Paris. He knows what he can do. Madrid does not.

N: His name is Pablo Picasso, and no one here has heard of him. He was born in Málaga, on the Andalusian coast you have just travelled through.

N: He has a plan. With a friend, he starts a magazine to shake up the city. It is called Young Art. And he paints. Night after night he paints the women of Madrid's cafés and backstreets, the ones respectable people pretend not to see.

## The big chance

...

N: Every few years, Madrid holds a great official exhibition. Ministers come. Critics come. Medals are handed out. A painter who is noticed there can make a career in a single afternoon.

N: The young man chooses his strongest picture. This one. He sends it in. And he waits for Madrid to notice him.

N: So what happened?

...

N: Look at her first. Find her face. Chalk-white powder. Red lips. Eyes that slide away from yours. She is dressed up for someone, but it is not you.

...

N: Now look at the dress. See how fast the brush moves, how loose it is. There is a defiance in it. He is not painting a lady. He is painting a woman who knows exactly what she is selling, and how little the world thinks of her.

N: And look at that colour. Blue. Within months, he will paint almost nothing but blue, the years of beggars, the blind, and grief. Here, the blue is still just a dress. It has not yet become a mood.

## Silence

...

N: Here is the answer. Nothing happened. Nobody noticed. The museum's own record says it simply went unnoticed.

N: No medal. No review. No buyer. By the time the exhibition closed, the young man had given up on Madrid and gone back to Paris. He never came back for his painting.

A: There is a sharper version of the story. They say it was turned down for a prize, and that the proud young Picasso was so stung he never went to collect it. Maybe. It is the kind of thing he would do. But that part is legend.

...

N: The painting was left behind. For decades it lay forgotten. Nobody guessed who the boy had become. Only years later did someone look again and realise what they had.

## What Madrid missed

...

N: Stand back now. Think of what it would have meant. If one critic had stopped in front of this picture, Picasso might have stayed in Spain. He did not. He went to Paris, and the century followed him there.

N: Today, a work by him from these early years is among the most sought-after paintings on earth. This one belongs to the nation, and will never be sold. Madrid ignored it for nothing, and now it is beyond price.

N: So stand with her a moment. She was painted to be looked at. For half a lifetime, no one did. Does she look like she is waiting?

...

N: He never lived in Spain again. But in this building, he comes home once more, in the painting at the end of our tour.

## Walk on

N: Now head deeper into this floor, to room two hundred and five point zero six. Check the map or ask a guard. That is stop two.
