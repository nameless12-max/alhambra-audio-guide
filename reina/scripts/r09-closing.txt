# title: Leaving the Reina Sofía
# stop: 0

## Thirty-eight tonnes, missing

N: One last story before you go. It is about this museum, and something it lost.

N: Picture a warehouse on the edge of Madrid. Somewhere inside, there should be four blocks of rusted steel. Each one is about chest high. Together they weigh thirty-eight tonnes. You need a crane to move them.

N: The American sculptor Richard Serra made them for this museum when it first opened. He set them on the floor in two pairs, like a quiet argument. The title names two bombed places. Guernica, and an American air raid on Libya that same year.

N: After the show, the steel went into storage, with a private company. Years passed. Then a new director ordered a count of everything the museum owned. The company had gone bust years before. The steel was gone.

N: Thirty-eight tonnes of art had vanished.

...

A: Where did it go? Nobody knows. The favourite guess is that someone sold it for scrap, and it was melted down. That is only a guess, never proved. It is still one of the strangest losses in the art world.

...

N: Serra agreed to make the work again, exactly the same. But he and the museum signed a rule. Only one may exist at a time. There can never be two. If you have time, the new version is listed in room one hundred and two. Check the map.

## On your way out

...

N: As you leave, look up at the glass lift towers on the face of the old hospital. Ride one if you can, and watch the city drop away.

N: If you pass through the newer red building, step into its courtyard. A giant brushstroke stands there, by Roy Lichtenstein, a painter's swipe of paint, frozen solid and standing upright. After Guernica, it feels like a joke about painting itself. Let yourself smile.

## Goodbye

N: You have followed a painting from a burning market town to a Paris attic, across an ocean, and home again. Think of that mother, and the tiny flower by the soldier's hand.

N: And think of your own road, Monica. Amsterdam, then Lisbon, then the courts and fountains of the Alhambra, and now Madrid. Thank you for walking with me all the way. Enjoy Madrid.
