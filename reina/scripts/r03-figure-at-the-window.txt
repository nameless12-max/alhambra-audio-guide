# title: Stop 3. Dalí: Figure at the Window
# stop: 3
# room: 205.06

## The sister

N: Stop three. You are still in room two hundred and five point zero six. Find a young woman seen from behind, leaning on a window sill, looking out at a calm blue bay.

...

N: Picture a summer house on a rocky bay on the Catalan coast. Whitewashed walls. Fishing boats. A young man at an easel who cannot stop painting the same girl.

N: The girl is his sister. She is seventeen. He is twenty-one. Their mother has died a few years before, and the two of them have held on to each other. She sits for him again and again, a dozen times, maybe more. He paints her sewing, reading, staring out to sea.

N: He is Salvador Dalí. Not the Dalí of melting clocks and the upturned moustache. Not yet. This is the Dalí before all that.

## Look at the calm

...

N: Look at the picture. Find the curtain. It hangs perfectly straight. Now the sea. Flat, glassy, not a ripple. Find the boat on it. Now find her curls, each one painted with care.

N: Everything here is in order. Everything is quiet. It is a picture of a boy who loves his sister, painted as carefully as he knows how.

...

N: And now notice what he does not show you. Her face. She has her back to us. She is looking out, away from the room, away from him. It is as if he already knows she is somewhere he cannot follow.

## The guest

...

N: That same year, Dalí brings a friend home for the holidays. A poet from Granada, charming, musical, impossible not to like. Federico García Lorca.

N: He fills the house. He recites, he plays the piano, he makes everyone laugh. Dalí's sister is dazzled.

A: And here the gossip begins. They say the sister fell for the poet, and that the poet fell for her brother. All three may have been a little in love at once. Nobody knows for sure. That is what makes the house at the bay so haunting.

...

## The woman who came between them

N: A few summers later, another guest arrives. A Russian woman, older than Dalí, married to a French poet. Her name is Gala. You will meet her at stop five.

N: Dalí falls for her completely. His father is outraged, and throws his son out of the family house. The sister has to choose. She chooses her father.

...

N: Twenty years go by. Dalí is now world famous, and Gala is at his side. And the sister publishes a book. Its title is simply Salvador Dalí, seen by his sister. In it she describes the quiet, sweet brother she remembers, the boy at the easel, before Gala and before the fame.

N: Dalí reads it as an attack. He feels his wife has been insulted. He cuts his sister off. They will go almost forty years barely speaking. Two people who once spent whole summers in one room together.

## The window

...

N: Look at her again. A girl leaning on a sill, the sea flat and blue, her brother behind her with a brush. She does not turn round.

...

N: She never would again.
