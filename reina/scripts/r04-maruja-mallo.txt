# title: Stop 4. Maruja Mallo: The Fair
# stop: 4
# room: 205.11

## Four friends and no hats

N: Stop four. You are in room two hundred and five point eleven, on the second floor. Look for a crowded, spinning fairground, full of rides, stalls and very odd characters.

...

A: Let me start with a story she told herself, years later. Picture the Puerta del Sol, the beating heart of Madrid, one ordinary day in the nineteen twenties. Crowds. Trams. Every man in a hat, every woman in a hat. That is simply how decent people go out.

A: Four young friends walk into the square. Two young women painters. And two young men you have already met, Salvador Dalí and Federico García Lorca.

A: In the middle of the square, they stop. And together, they take off their hats.

...

A: In her telling, the crowd turns on them. Insults. Jeers. Even stones. As if four bare heads were an attack on Spain itself. It may have grown in the retelling. But she told it with delight for the rest of her life.

N: The story stuck so well that today the women of that generation have a nickname. The women without hats. Painters, poets, thinkers who refused to cover up, and who were then left out of the history books for half a century. This whole room is about bringing them back.

## The girl from the edge

...

N: The young woman who told that story is Maruja Mallo. She comes from a small town on the wild Atlantic coast of Galicia, and she arrives in Madrid hungry for everything. The art school. The cafés. The night. The street.

N: And she has a gift for getting noticed. Within a few years, the most powerful thinker in Spain, the philosopher Ortega y Gasset, organises a show of her paintings. A young woman painter, given that stage. It is almost unheard of.

N: This picture was in it.

## Into the fair

...

N: Now let yourself fall into it. This is a summer street fair in Madrid, a verbena. Imagine the noise. Barrel organs, shouting stallholders, the smell of fried dough.

...

N: Find the shooting gallery. Now find the machine where young men try to prove their strength, slamming it to ring the bell.

...

N: Now look harder. Find the giant with one eye. Find the priest, of all people, at a sideshow. Find the beggar with his guitar.

N: Nobody here is quite respectable. Not the church, not the soldiers, not the fine ladies. She has invited them all to her fair, and then quietly made fun of every one of them. Can you feel the laugh hiding in it?

## Exile

...

N: Then the war comes and the laughing stops. Many of her friends are killed or scattered. Lorca, the young man who took off his hat beside her, is shot.

N: She escapes Spain, and crosses the ocean to South America. She will not live in Madrid again for decades.

N: When she finally returns, almost nobody remembers her. So she makes them remember. She walks the streets of Madrid in dramatic make-up and outrageous clothes, the oldest rebel in town. And a new generation of young artists finds her, and adores her.

## The last laugh

...

N: Last year, this museum gave her the largest show of her work ever held. The girl the crowd once jeered in the Puerta del Sol came back, and filled the walls.

...

N: Look at the fair one last time. All that noise, all that colour, all those pompous people spinning round and round.

N: Who do you think is having the most fun here? The crowd? Or the woman who painted them?

...

N: Before you leave, look around the room. You may find more work by Ángeles Santos, the girl of stop two, in her darker years.
