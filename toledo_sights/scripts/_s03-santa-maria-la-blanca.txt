# title: Stop 3. Santa María la Blanca: The forest of white arches
# stop: 3
# room: Jewish quarter, Calle de los Reyes Católicos

## Three faiths in one room

N: Stop three. Santa María la Blanca, on Calle de los Reyes Católicos, behind a plain wall and a small garden. Go in and stand at the back, by the door, before you look at anything.

...

N: Now look. Rows of white horseshoe arches, one after another, marching away from you. Thick white pillars. Soft light. It feels like a forest in snow.

N: Hold a question while you look. Who built this, and for whom? Because the answer is the most Toledo thing in Toledo.

## The answer

N: The arches are the shape you know from Córdoba, from the great mosque. The builders were Muslim craftsmen, working in their own tradition.

N: But it was never a mosque. It was built, around eight hundred years ago, as a synagogue. Probably the main synagogue of Toledo's Jews.

N: And the land it stood in was a Christian kingdom. Castile. Its king had taken Toledo a century before.

...

N: So. Muslim hands. A Jewish prayer house. A Christian king's permission. All three, in one room. This is the dream people mean when they talk about the three cultures of Toledo. Look at it closely, because the dream is about to break.

## Look at the pillars

N: First, walk to one of the pillars and look at its top. The capital. Carved plaster, white. Look for the pinecones. Scrolls and pinecones twisting together, each one slightly different.

N: Then look up above the arches, at the round panels and the band of decoration. Patterns, not pictures. In a synagogue, as in a mosque, nobody wanted images of people on the walls.

...

## The breaking

N: In thirteen ninety-one, a wave of riots against the Jews swept through Castile and beyond. Mobs attacked Jewish quarters. Thousands were killed or forced to convert. Toledo was not spared.

N: A few years later a famous preacher, Vicente Ferrer, came through, preaching that Jews must convert. Soon after, this synagogue was taken and made into a church. Santa María la Blanca. Saint Mary the White.

N: For the Church, a synagogue turned into a church was a sign of victory, of the old faith giving way to the new. For the Jews of Toledo, it was the loss of their house of prayer.

...

N: Then the room had many lives. A refuge for women who had left prostitution. Later, a barracks for soldiers. Later still, a storehouse. The white arches held soldiers and stores, and never fell.

## Who it belongs to

A: There is a doubt in this room that has not gone away. The building still belongs to the Church. It is a museum now, not a synagogue, and not a parish church either. In twenty thirteen, the Jewish community of Toledo asked for it back. As far as we know, nothing has changed.

...

N: Stand in the middle of the five aisles. Look down the rows of arches one last time. Imagine the voices that filled them, in Hebrew, every Sabbath, for two hundred years. Then imagine the quiet that came after.

## Walk on

N: Walk on, a few steps up the street, to the great stone monastery with chains on its walls. That is stop four, San Juan de los Reyes.
