# title: Stop 6. Toledo: The city of three cultures
# stop: 6
# room: Anywhere, ideally a viewpoint over the river

## The postcard and the truth

N: Stop six, the last. Play this wherever you are. If you can, find a spot where you can see the river bending round the rock.

...

N: You will hear it everywhere in Toledo. The city of three cultures. Christians, Muslims and Jews, side by side. It is on the posters and in the shops. Is it true? The honest answer is yes, and no.

## Yes

N: Think of what you saw today. A mosque whose builder signed his name in brick. A church with Arabic letters round its arches. And in the Jewish quarter you walked through, two synagogues still stand, one built by Muslim craftsmen, one decorated like a palace of Granada.

N: In this city, scholars of three faiths worked together, turning books from Arabic into Latin and Castilian. Astronomy, medicine, philosophy, much of it Greek learning kept alive in Arabic, came back into Europe through Toledo. That was real. It made this city.

## No

...

N: But living side by side is not the same as being equal. Jews and Muslims lived under Christian law, protected in good times, and in bad times by nobody. In thirteen ninety-one, riots swept the Jewish quarters of Castile. Synagogues were taken.

N: Then, fourteen ninety-two. The year Granada fell. That spring, Isabella and Ferdinand signed the order expelling every Jew who would not convert. They signed it in the Alhambra. The palace you walked through.

...

N: Families who had lived here longer than anyone could remember had a few months to go. Many left, for Portugal, North Africa, the lands of the Ottomans. Their descendants kept speaking a form of Spanish for five hundred years. The Muslims of Castile were forced to convert a few years later, and a century after that, their descendants were expelled too.

A: Some Sephardic families are said to have carried the keys of their Spanish houses with them, and kept them for generations. It is hard to prove. The story has never died.

## What is left

N: So the three cultures are here, in the stone. The people who made it are mostly gone. The beauty is real. The loss is real. Toledo asks you to carry both.

...

## Goodbye, Toledo

N: Look at the river one last time, wrapped round the city like an arm.

N: Thank you for walking it with me, Monica. Take the train back to Madrid. And if you go back to the Prado, find El Greco again. You have stood in his city now, in front of his son. His saints will look different.

...

N: Goodbye, Toledo.
