# title: Stop 4. San Román: Arabic on a Christian wall
# stop: 4
# room: Museum of the Councils and Visigothic Culture, Calle San Román

## A church that speaks two languages

N: Stop four. The church of San Román, on the high ground at the centre of the old town. These days it is the Museum of the Councils and Visigothic Culture. Go in, stand in the middle of the nave, and look at the arches first.

...

N: Horseshoe arches, in rows, like the ones you just saw at the little mosque. Now look at the columns holding them up. They do not match. Some are older than the church by many centuries, taken from Visigothic and even Roman buildings and set up again here.

N: But this was never a mosque. It was built as a church, by Christians, in the Mudéjar way: the building style of Muslim craftsmen, working for Christian patrons.

## The victory painted on the walls

N: Hold a question. Why would a Christian archbishop want Arabic letters in his church?

N: Here is the scene. Early in the thirteenth century, a Christian army has just crushed a Muslim army in the south, at a place called Las Navas de Tolosa. The Archbishop of Toledo rode with that army. Back home, he has this church painted and consecrated again, to celebrate.

...

N: Now look up at the walls. The paintings are in two bands, one above the other, with a strip of lettering running between them. Look for the four evangelists, each with wings. Look for a row of bishops, the archbishops of Toledo. Look for two young saints in deacon's robes, Stephen and Lawrence.

N: And look around the arches for more bands of writing. Some of it is Latin, in verse. Some of it is in Arabic letters.

N: One answer to the question: in Toledo, Arabic was not only the enemy's script. It was the look of beauty and learning, the way fine buildings were dressed. So a victory over the Muslim south was painted in a style that came from it.

A: Scholars still argue over what some of that Arabic says, and whether it was meant to be read at all. Perhaps it was there simply to look splendid.

## The end of the world

...

N: Now find the Last Judgement. In Christian belief it is the day the dead rise from their graves to be judged. See if you can find them, small figures rising to face their judge. In a church built to give thanks for a battle, the painters put the end of the world. Everyone, winner and loser, will answer in the end.

## The boy king in the tower

A: Before you go further, a story about the tower outside. Toledo tells that in a time of civil war, when the city was held by rivals, a child king of Castile was brought in by surprise, and shown to the people from the tower of San Román. The city rose for him, and his enemies fled. That boy grew up to be the king who won at Las Navas. The details vary from one teller to the next. But it is a good tale to have in your head when you look up at that tower.

## The kings before the Moors

N: Now walk round the cases. Before the Muslims came, Toledo was the capital of the Visigoths, a Germanic people who ruled Spain from here for well over a century. Their bishops met in great councils in this city, and at one of them a king gave up his old faith and took the Catholic one. That is why the museum carries the councils' name.

N: Look for the belt buckles, the jewellery, the carved stones.

N: And look for the copies of the gold crowns of Guarrazar. Check at the door if you cannot find them.

A: The story goes that one summer in the eighteen fifties, after heavy rain near a village outside Toledo, a farming couple found gold glinting in the earth. It was a buried hoard of crowns and crosses, hidden, it is thought, when the Muslims arrived. Pieces were sold off quietly, some to France.

N: The originals are now in Madrid and Paris. Kings hung them over altars as gifts to God. On the finest, gold letters hang from the rim, spelling out a king's name and the words "offers this". If the copies here include it, find those letters.

N: One crown, of another king, was stolen from the royal armoury in Madrid in the twentieth century. It has never been found.

...

N: Stand under the arches once more. Roman columns. Visigothic gold. Arabic letters. Christian saints, and the Last Judgement. One small church, holding every Toledo at once.

## Walk on

N: Walk on, downhill, to the cathedral. That is stop five.
