# title: Stop 1. El Tránsito: The treasurer's synagogue
# stop: 1
# room: Jewish quarter, Calle Samuel Leví

## A house of prayer next to a rich man's house

N: Stop one. The Synagogue of El Tránsito, on Calle Samuel Leví, in the Jewish quarter. Step inside the great prayer hall and walk to the middle of the floor.

...

N: Good morning, Monica. Toledo starts here, with a man who had everything, and lost it.

N: Picture this street in the time of Pedro, King of Castile. His enemies called him Pedro the Cruel. His friends called him Pedro the Just. Both had reasons.

N: The king needs money, always. And the man who finds it for him is a Jew from an old Toledo family. Samuel ha-Leví. Treasurer of the kingdom. One of the most powerful men in Castile.

N: Samuel lives in a fine house on this spot. And beside his house, with his own money, he builds this. A private synagogue, so splendid that the whole city must have talked about it.

N: Church law said Jews could not build new synagogues. The clergy objected. The king let him do it anyway. That is how high Samuel stood.

## Read the walls

...

N: Now look up, to the band of carved plaster that runs right round the top of the walls. Lace, cut in stone-hard plaster. Leaves, stars, knots.

N: Inside that lace there is writing. Hebrew letters, large and square, running along the walls. And between them, Arabic-style script used purely as ornament.

N: The Hebrew inscriptions praise God. Then they praise the king, Pedro. And then they praise the builder, Samuel ha-Leví, a prince among the Levites. A man carving his loyalty into the walls, so that the king and God would both see it.

...

N: Does it remind you of somewhere? It should. Only days ago you stood under plaster like this in the Alhambra. Pedro was an ally of the sultan of Granada, the man who built the Court of the Lions. His palace in Seville was built in the same style, it is thought partly by craftsmen from Granada. Here the same art of the Muslim south decorates a Jewish house of prayer, for a Christian king.

## The fall

N: Now the turn. A treasurer is only safe while the king trusts him.

N: Around thirteen sixty, Pedro turns on Samuel. He suspects him of hiding money. Samuel is arrested, taken to Seville, and tortured. He dies there.

A: The legend in Toledo says he died rather than tell where his treasure was hidden. One plaque in the city says just that. Other accounts say he confessed under torture, and his family suffered with him. Nobody now can be sure. But the walls he built still speak his name.

...

N: Pedro did not last much longer. Nine years later he was killed by his own half-brother, in a fight inside a tent. The brother took the throne.

## What happened to the room

N: A century and more later came the expulsion of the Jews from Spain. The synagogue passed to a military order of knights and became a church, named for the passing of the Virgin. El Tránsito. That is why a synagogue has a Christian name.

N: Today it is the Sephardic Museum. The upstairs gallery, where women once prayed, and the side rooms, hold what survived of Jewish Spain. Gravestones. Wedding clothes. Objects carried far away and brought back.

...

N: Stand once more under the inscriptions. A man wrote his love for his king into these walls. The king killed him. The walls are still here.

## Walk on

N: When you are ready, the house of El Greco is a few steps away. But first, his greatest picture. That is stop two, Santo Tomé.
