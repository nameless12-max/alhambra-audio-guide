# title: Stop 5. The Cathedral: Six things not to miss
# stop: 5
# room: Cathedral of Toledo, Plaza del Ayuntamiento

## How to use this stop

N: Stop five. The cathedral of Toledo. Six things, six stories, in any order. Pause between them, find the next one, and press play.

...

N: This was the seat of the most powerful churchman in Spain, the Archbishop of Toledo. Everything here was made by people with money, who wanted to be seen spending it on God.

## One. A hole in the roof

N: Walk round behind the high altar, into the passage at the back. Stop when the light changes. Then turn, and look up.

...

N: Marble clouds. Angels tumbling. Rays of gold. And above it all, real daylight, pouring through a hole in the ceiling.

N: This is the Transparente. Behind the high altar the bread of the Mass was kept, the most holy thing in the church. But it sat in the dark. So, in the seventeen hundreds, a sculptor named Narciso Tomé did something outrageous. He cut a hole in the Gothic vault of this cathedral and let the sun in.

N: Some people at the time were thrilled. Some were horrified. Which are you? Find the Last Supper, carved high up in the marble, and watch how the light lands on it.

## Two. The red robe

N: Now go to the sacristy. Walk to the far end of the long room. The painting on the end wall.

...

N: El Greco's Disrobing of Christ. One of his first commissions in Toledo. If you saw his saints at the Prado, this is where his Spanish story starts.

N: Look at the robe. That red. It burns out of a crowd of grey faces and steel. Christ looks up. The soldiers close in. Someone is already reaching for the cloth.

N: The cathedral did not like it. They argued about the price, and it went to valuation. And, as the story goes, they objected to the three Marys in the corner, who are not in the Gospel at this moment. Look at the three women, lower left. They stayed.

N: A young foreigner, fighting the richest church in Spain. The picture has hung here ever since.

## Three. The war in wood

N: Now walk to the choir, the enclosed space in the middle of the nave. Look at the lower row of wooden seats, and above each seat, the carved panel.

...

N: Every panel is a scene from one war. The conquest of Granada. Towns falling. Walls. Ladders. Turbaned defenders on the battlements. Knights with banners riding in.

N: A carver named Rodrigo Alemán made them, only a few years after the war ended. More than fifty scenes. The war was still news.

N: You have just walked through the Alhambra, the prize at the end of this war. Find one panel and look at the people on the walls. Someone is about to lose their city.

## Four. A tower of silver

N: Now find the Treasury chapel. Behind the glass, a tower taller than a person, in silver and gold. The great monstrance, the Custodia.

N: It was made to carry the holy bread through the streets at Corpus Christi, and still goes out each year.

...

N: Look into its heart. At the centre there is a smaller piece, in gold. That one belonged to Queen Isabella. After her death, the cathedral acquired it, and the goldsmith built this great tower around it.

A: The guides love a story. The gilding, they say, was done with the first gold Columbus brought back from the Americas. Nobody has proved it. But look at the shine, and think of where gold came from in those years.

## Five. The room of the archbishops

N: Now find the Chapter House, off the walkway behind the high altar. The room where the canons met to govern. Look up first.

...

N: A ceiling of carved and painted wood, gold and colour, in the Mudéjar style. Then the walls, painted all over by Juan de Borgoña. Scenes from the life of the Virgin. And at the far end, the Last Judgement. A message for the men who sat here: you will answer for what you decide in this room.

N: Now look lower, along the walls, under the frescoes. A long row of painted faces. These are the archbishops of Toledo, one after another, reaching back toward the earliest centuries. The early faces are imagined; there was nothing to copy.

N: The man who built this room was Cardinal Cisneros, archbishop, regent of Spain, the queen's confessor. A row of predecessors around the room where he ruled. He was telling everyone who walked in that he stood at the end of a line more than a thousand years long.

## Six. The old Christians

N: Last, find the Mozarabic Chapel, near the main west doors. It may be closed outside its service; check at the door.

N: When Toledo was a Muslim city, its Christians kept their own old way of worship, different from Rome's. They were called Mozarabs. When Christian kings took Toledo, the new rulers wanted the Roman way instead.

A: Now the legend. The king, they say, ordered a trial by fire. Two prayer books were thrown into the flames, the Roman and the Toledan. The Toledan book would not burn. And so the old rite was allowed to survive.

N: That is legend. What is true is this. Cardinal Cisneros, the man of the Chapter House, founded this chapel to keep the old rite alive. It is still celebrated here.

...

N: Of all the gold in this building, that may be the rarest thing. A way of praying older than the cathedral, still spoken inside it.

## Walk on

N: When you are ready, the closing track is stop six. Play it wherever you are.
