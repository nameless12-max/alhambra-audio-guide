# title: Stop 3. The Choir: A war carved while it was still being fought
# stop: 3
# room: The choir, enclosed in the middle of the nave

## News from the front

N: Stop three. The choir, the walled room in the middle of the nave where the canons sang. Step in. Two rows of wooden seats run round you, one high, one low. Go close to the lower row, and look at the carved panels above the backs of the seats, at about the height of your chest.

...

N: Picture a workshop in Toledo. A German woodcarver, Rodrigo Alemán, has just been given a strange order by the canons. Carve the war.

N: The war is the war of Granada. The last Muslim kingdom in Spain. Isabella and Ferdinand are taking it town by town, castle by castle. And it is not over. When the carver starts, the Alhambra is still Muslim. News comes in. Another town has fallen. A new panel is begun.

N: Fifty-four panels. Most of them, a historian worked out, can be matched to a real town or castle, often by a name carved on them.

...

N: Look closely at one. Walls and towers like a child's castle. Ladders. Knights in armour, with banners. And on the walls, the defenders in turbans. Look for a gate opening. A surrender.

N: Now find a face on the losing side. You have walked through the Alhambra. These are the people who lost it. Imagine one of them about to give up the city where his grandparents are buried.

...

## The carver's jokes

N: Now look down, under the seats. Folded up, each seat shows a small ledge, so an old canon could lean while seeming to stand. On these ledges and on the armrests, look for animals, monsters, odd little faces.

A: Rodrigo Alemán had a name for mischief. In another cathedral, at Plasencia, his carvings under the seats are famous for being rude. And Plasencia tells a legend about him. That he was shut up in a tower, built himself wings, and jumped. It is only a story.

...

## The duel upstairs

N: Now look up at the high row of seats, behind and above you. A generation later, another archbishop wanted the upper stalls. He did not hire one master. He hired two, and gave each one half.

N: On one side, Felipe Bigarny, from Burgundy, the old master. On the other, Alonso Berruguete, a Spaniard who had been to Italy as a young man, and had seen Michelangelo at work. The old style against the new.

...

N: Compare the two sides. Bigarny's figures are calm, balanced. Berruguete's twist and strain, as if the wood could not hold them. Which side is whose? Look at which side makes you nervous. That is Berruguete. The old master died before the work was done.

N: At the far end, the archbishop's throne, and above it Berruguete's Transfiguration, Christ rising in light. He took the place of honour.

## The White Virgin

N: Last, look for a stone statue of the Virgin and Child, painted white, standing on the altar inside the choir.

...

N: Look at the mother, then at the child. Their faces turn to each other. Is she smiling? Decide for yourself. She was already famous in the fourteenth century, more than a hundred years before the war around her was carved.

...

N: A mother and a baby, in the middle of a war.

## Walk on

N: Walk on. Leave the choir and cross to the high altar, behind the tall iron grille. Play stop four.
