# title: Stop 7. The Chapel of Santiago: The favourite who lost his head
# stop: 7
# room: Capilla de Santiago, off the ambulatory, beside the central chapel of Saint Ildefonsus

## The man who ruled the king

N: Stop seven. The chapel of Santiago. Look through the grille. In the middle, two tombs of white alabaster side by side. A man in armour. A woman beside him. Both with their hands joined in prayer.

...

N: The man is Álvaro de Luna. For three decades he was the most powerful man in Castile. More powerful than the king.

N: He had come to court as a boy, and the boy king took to him. The king made him Constable of Castile, then Master of the Order of Santiago, with its castles and its armies. The great lords hated him for it. Again and again they rose against him. Again and again he won.

N: And he built this chapel as his own tomb, in the cathedral of the first city of Castile. A grand chapel, with his family's crescent moons in the stone. Look for them.

...

## The statue that knelt

A: Now the legend. On his first tomb here, they say, Don Álvaro placed a figure of himself in gilded bronze. And it had a secret. During Mass, by some hidden mechanism, the bronze man rose from his tomb, and knelt, and prayed.

N: That it moved is legend. That there was a gilded bronze statue is recorded. And its end is recorded too. When his enemies took Toledo, they had it pulled down and melted. When Don Álvaro heard, he is reported to have laughed. There is no point, he said, in taking revenge on a statue.

N: The enemies would get the man himself instead.

## A basin for coins

N: In the end it was the queen. The king's second wife distrusted the favourite, and she worked on her husband. And one spring the king gave way. The man who had ruled for him for thirty years was arrested, tried in haste, and condemned.

N: Picture the main square of Valladolid, an early summer day. A scaffold. He climbs it. And afterwards his head is set high on a post and left there for days, for the crowd to see.

N: An old account adds one more thing. His body lay on the scaffold beside a basin. A basin for coins, collected from passers by, to pay for his burial. The richest man in Castile, buried on charity.

...

N: And the queen who brought him down? She was the mother of Isabella, then a baby of two. The queen of the choir stalls and the silver tower.

## The sad countess

N: His widow, Juana Pimentel, signed her letters after that as the sad countess. She, and later their daughter, finished this chapel and brought him here. The tombs you see were made decades after his death.

N: Look at the corners of the tombs. Small kneeling figures keep watch, at his, knights of his order; at hers, friars.

...

N: And look at his face. Calm, eyes closed, hands together. No hint of the scaffold. His family carved him the death he did not have.

## Walk on

N: Walk on round the ambulatory, past more chapels, toward the far side of the church. Look for the large chapel of the New Kings, and beyond it the door of the sacristy. Go into the sacristy. Play stop eight.
