# title: Stop 8. The Sacristy: El Greco against the cathedral
# stop: 8
# room: Sacristy and picture gallery, off the left side of the ambulatory as you face the high altar

## The young foreigner

N: Stop eight. The sacristy. A long, high hall hung with paintings. Walk to the far end and stand before the painting on the end wall. A man in a robe of blazing red, in a crowd of grey faces and steel.

...

N: Toledo, the late fifteen seventies. A Greek painter in his thirties has just arrived from Italy. Few in Spain know his name; they will soon call him simply the Greek. Through a friend from Rome, the dean's son, he gets his first great Spanish commission. A painting for this room. Christ, about to be stripped of his clothes before the crucifixion.

N: He gives them this.

...

N: Look at the red. It is the loudest colour in the cathedral. Around him, helmets and faces pushing in. Find the hands already reaching for the robe. And at the lower right, find the man bent over, drilling holes in the cross, getting it ready.

## The quarrel

N: The canons did not like it. And when the time came to agree a price, they said so, in writing.

N: First, look at the heads. The heads of the tormentors rise above the head of Christ. Find them. To the canons, that was an insult. Nobody should stand higher than Christ.

N: Second, look at the lower left. Three women. The three Marys. The canons pointed out, correctly, that the Gospel does not put them here at this moment. Remove them, they said.

...

N: The painter's own expert valued the picture at nine hundred and fifty ducats. The cathedral fought it. He was awarded three hundred and fifty. Little more than a third.

N: And the figures? He was supposed to change them. Look again at the three women and at the heads above Christ. He never did. They are still there.

...

N: A young foreigner, against the richest church in Spain. He lost the money, and kept the painting exactly as he wanted it. If you saw his saints in the Prado, this is where that Spanish story began.

## The rest of the room

N: Now turn round, and look up. The ceiling is painted with a heaven full of figures, by Luca Giordano. Find the scene in it. The Virgin coming down to give Saint Ildefonsus his robe. The legend from stop one.

N: Along the walls, look for a set of apostles, by the Greek again. And look for Goya. A dark, crowded Arrest of Christ, a crush of faces, from the painter of the Prado's black, haunted pictures. There may be a Titian and a Caravaggio in the rooms beside; check the labels.

...

## Dust, ashes, nothing

N: Beside the sacristy is the chapel of the Sagrario. If you can see in, or check the floor plan, it holds the grave of Cardinal Portocarrero.

N: He stood by the bed of the last, sickly Habsburg king of Spain as he was dying without a child, and pressed him to leave his crown to a French prince. The king did. A war across Europe followed, for more than ten years.

N: And on his own gravestone, the cardinal ordered these words, in Latin. Here lies dust, ashes, and nothing.

## Walk on

N: Walk on. The last stop is the cloister. The way in is on this side of the church, toward the bell tower; follow the signs, or check the floor plan. Play stop nine.
