# title: Stop 5. The Chapter House: A room that watches you decide
# stop: 5
# room: Sala Capitular, off the right-hand side of the ambulatory

## The men who ran the money

N: Stop five. The chapter house. You enter through a small antechamber, then the hall. Before anything else, stop in the doorway of the hall and look up.

...

N: A ceiling of carved wood, gold and colour, set in deep boxes, like the inside of a jewel case. It was made by Toledan carpenters in the Mudéjar way, the craft Christian Spain learned from its Muslim builders, the craft of the Alhambra ceilings. And it was made only a few years after Granada fell.

N: This is where the canons met. The chapter. Picture them, a few dozen men in black, seated on the benches round these walls. They managed the income of the richest church in Spain. They hired the painters and the carvers. They argued with the archbishop. You have already met them saying no, to a cardinal who wanted a tomb among the kings.

## The Judgement on the wall

N: Now look at the walls above the benches. Painted all over, by Juan de Borgoña, the leading painter of Toledo in Cisneros's day. Scenes from the life of the Virgin. Find the end wall.

...

N: The Last Judgement. Souls rising from their graves. Some lifted to heaven. Some dragged down to hell. Look at the faces of the damned.

N: Think about where it is. Not in a chapel for the people. In the boardroom. Every time these men voted on money or on a fight with the archbishop, this was in front of them. You will answer for what you decide in this room.

## A thousand years of faces

N: Now look lower, along the walls, under the paintings. A long row of portraits, one after another, like a family album. Every archbishop of Toledo, in order.

...

N: The oldest faces are invented. Those men lived in Roman and Visigothic times, and nobody knew what they looked like.

N: Then, as you walk along, the faces become real people. Look for one in particular. A lean, old man, seen in profile, with a long nose and a hollow cheek, in a plain dark habit. Cardinal Cisneros. Check the names painted under the portraits.

...

N: This portrait is one of the few likenesses of him made in his lifetime. A friar who walked barefoot, who slept on boards, they said, under his silk. Who ran Spain twice as regent. The man behind the gold altar, the silver tower, the old rite, and this room.

N: And he chose to paint himself into a line more than a thousand years long. He was telling anyone who entered: I am not one man. I am the latest in a chain that goes back to the saints.

## Still counting

N: Now go to the other end of the row. The series has never stopped. Each new archbishop has been added. Find the last face on the wall.

...

N: Every archbishop who sits in this room knows that one day his own face will join the line.

## Walk on

N: Walk on. Go back out into the ambulatory, and continue round behind the high altar. Stop when the light changes, then turn and look up. Play stop six.
