# title: Stop 10. Goodbye: Everyone wanted to be remembered
# stop: 10
# room: Anywhere; the cloister garden is a good place

## What they left behind

N: Stop ten, the last. Sit, if you can. Or step outside and look back at the tower.

...

N: Think of the people you met today. Not the saints. The people.

N: A queen and a monk who broke a king's promise while he was away. A Muslim jurist who begged for peace, and was given a place on a pillar inside the mosque he lost.

N: A German carver who cut a war into walnut while the war was still being fought.

N: A cardinal who wanted to lie among kings, and a queen who knocked down a wall for him. A friar who slept on boards and built in gold. A favourite who ruled a king for decades, and was buried with coins from a basin. A young Greek who lost his fee, and kept his three women in the picture.

N: And an archbishop who cut a hole in the roof to let the sun in.

...

## Dust and ashes

N: Almost all of them wanted the same thing. To be remembered. They put their faces in the chapter house, their arms on the grilles, their bodies in the best places they could buy or fight for.

N: Only one of them, the man under the stone that says dust, ashes, and nothing, pretended not to care. And even he made sure the words were carved.

N: Here is the question to take out with you. Of everything in this building, what will you remember? A gold altar? A bell that rang once? Or one face, in wood, on the walls of a town about to fall?

...

## Goodbye

N: Monica, you have walked through the Alhambra, the prize at the end of the war in the choir. You stood in front of El Greco in Madrid. And today you have seen where his Spanish story started, with a quarrel over a red robe.

N: Thank you for walking it with me, all the way.

N: When you leave, go out the way the signs show you. Step into the street, and look up one last time at the single tower. The other one was never built. The cathedral is still, in its way, unfinished.

N: Goodbye.
