# title: Stop 6. The Transparente: The archbishop who cut a hole in the roof
# stop: 6
# room: Ambulatory, directly behind the high altar

## The problem in the dark

N: Stop six. You are in the walkway behind the high altar. On one side, a wall of marble and bronze, swirling up toward the roof. Do not look at it yet. Look up first, at the vault above your head, on the opposite side.

...

N: There is a hole. A great opening, cut through the stone vault of a Gothic cathedral, framed with painted clouds and figures. Daylight pours through it.

N: Here is why. On the other side of this wall, inside the high altar, the cathedral kept the holy bread of the Mass in its tabernacle. And it sat in the dark, behind the huge gilded altarpiece you saw in stop four.

N: An archbishop, Diego de Astorga, wanted that to change. He wanted light to fall on the holy bread, and he wanted people behind the altar to be able to see through to it. Hence the name. The Transparente. The see-through.

## The sculptor and his sons

N: He gave the job to the cathedral's architect, Narciso Tomé, a sculptor who worked with his sons. They worked for years. Then they did the thing no medieval builder would have dared. They cut through the vault of the ambulatory and let the sky in.

...

N: Now turn and look at the marble. Stand back. Clouds of stone. Angels tumbling down on rays of gilded bronze. In the middle, an oval opening, the window to the tabernacle, ringed with angels. Above it, find a table and figures around it. The Last Supper, carved in marble, floating in mid air.

...

## Eighth wonder, or a monster

N: When it was unveiled, the city held festivities. A poet called it the eighth wonder of the world. It cost a fortune, two hundred thousand ducats, it is said.

N: Then taste changed. Later critics, who loved the calm lines of ancient Greece and Rome, found it overloaded and theatrical. People still argue.

...

N: Which are you? Stand there a moment and decide.

N: The archbishop got what he wanted in one more way. Look at the floor in front of it, or check the floor plan. He is buried here, at the foot of his own work, under the light he let in.

## Hats hanging from the sky

N: Now look up again, into the vaults round about, and into the chapels opening off this walkway. Look for something small and red, hanging on cords high above the floor. A wide, flat hat with tassels.

...

N: These are cardinals' hats. When a cardinal of Toledo was buried, his red hat was hung from the vault above his tomb.

A: And the guides of Toledo tell what happens next. The hat stays there, gathering dust, rotting slowly. And when at last it falls to the floor, they say, the cardinal's soul is free of purgatory, and goes up to heaven.

N: That is a saying, not doctrine. Count how many hats are still hanging on.

## Walk on

N: Walk on round the ambulatory. Just behind you is the central chapel of Saint Ildefonsus, with a cardinal's tomb in the middle. Next to it, the chapel of Santiago, with two white tombs side by side. Find that one. Play stop seven.
