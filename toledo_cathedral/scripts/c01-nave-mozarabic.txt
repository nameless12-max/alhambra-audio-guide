# title: Stop 1. The Nave: A giant, a stone, and a book that would not burn
# stop: 1
# room: South aisle by the entrance, then west along the nave to the Mozarabic chapel

## The man who keeps you alive today

N: Stop one. You are just inside. Somewhere on the wall near the Lions' Door, the great carved door at the end of the cross aisle, there is a painted man as tall as a house. A giant wading through water with a child on his shoulder. Find him.

...

N: Saint Christopher, the ferryman who carried the Christ child across a river.

A: Why so big? Because of a belief. In the Middle Ages, people said that if you looked at Saint Christopher, you would not die a sudden death that day. So churches painted him huge, by the doors. You have just looked. You are safe until midnight.

...

## The night the Virgin came down

N: Now walk west along the nave, toward the main doors, under the forest of pillars. Look for a small chapel built between two pillars, standing almost free in the aisle, with a little roof of carved stone. Check the floor plan if you cannot see it.

...

N: Thirteen centuries ago, the archbishop of this city was a scholar named Ildefonsus. He wrote a fierce book in honour of the Virgin Mary.

A: And this is what Toledo tells. One night, before dawn, he came into his church to sing the morning prayers. The Virgin was seated on his chair, surrounded by angels. She thanked him for his book, and laid on his shoulders a robe made in heaven.

N: That is legend. This chapel stands where the altar of the old Visigothic church is believed to have stood. Behind its grille the cathedral keeps a stone. The stone, they say, on which the Virgin set her feet.

...

N: Find it through the grille. A plain piece of rock, in a building of gold.

## The book that would not burn

N: Now carry on to the far corner, by the main doors, under the tower that was never finished. It may be open only for its morning service; look through the bars.

...

N: For nearly four hundred years Toledo was a Muslim city. Its Christians stayed, and kept praying in their own old way, in the words of the Visigoths. They were called Mozarabs. Then a Christian king took the city, and Rome wanted that old rite gone.

A: The legend says Toledo fought back. First, a duel. Two knights, one for the old rite, one for the Roman. The Toledan knight won. The king was not satisfied. So, a trial by fire. Both prayer books thrown on a bonfire. And the old Toledan book would not burn. But the king chose Rome anyway. They say that is where Spaniards got an old proverb. Laws go where kings want.

N: The legend is a later story. What is true is this. The old rite did not die. A few parishes kept it, barely. Four centuries later, Cardinal Cisneros, the hard, ascetic friar who ran Spain for Queen Isabella, had its books printed and built this chapel for it. Its mass is still said here every morning.

...

N: If you can see the walls, look for battle paintings. The conquest of Oran, in North Africa, a war Cisneros led himself, an old friar riding with the army.

N: A way of praying older than this building, kept alive inside it.

## Walk on

N: Walk on. Go to the opposite corner of the church, under the finished tower, to the treasury chapel. Play stop two.
