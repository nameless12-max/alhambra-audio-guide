# title: Stop 9. The Cloister and the Tower: A fire, and a bell that rang once
# stop: 9
# room: Lower cloister, on the north side; bell tower if your ticket includes it

## A market that burned

N: Stop nine. The cloister. Step out into the arcades round the garden. After the gold and the dark, open sky.

...

N: Before this cloister, there was no quiet here. There was a market. The alcaná, a tangle of shops and stalls pressed up against the cathedral, much of it run by Toledo's Jewish merchants. Picture the cloth, the shouting, the noise.

N: Then came an archbishop who wanted the ground. Pedro Tenorio. A hard man, a builder of bridges. He wanted a cloister worthy of his cathedral. The market was in the way.

A: And here Toledo tells a dark story. One night, it is said, the market caught fire. The shops burned. And with the market in ashes, the archbishop could buy the land. Who started the fire? The legend says it was set on purpose, to clear the way for him. Nobody has ever proved it.

N: What is recorded is that work on this cloister began in the late thirteen eighties, on the ground where the market had been. Two years later, in thirteen ninety-one, mobs attacked the Jewish quarters of Castile, Toledo among them. The quiet arcades you are standing in were built in that decade.

...

N: Look at the walls now. Large painted scenes, made centuries later, of Toledo's own saints and their lives. Find the one that catches you.

## The bell that rang once

N: Now look up, over the roofs, at the one finished tower of the cathedral. If your ticket includes the climb, ask the staff where it starts; it may be by time slot. About three hundred steps, a spiral, up to the clock and the bells.

...

N: Up there hangs the biggest of them all. Toledo calls her la Gorda. The fat one. She weighs more than seventeen tonnes, and is nearly three and a half metres across. In her day, one of the largest bells in Europe.

N: She was cast in the seventeen fifties. Imagine the city waiting for her voice. Then, the first time she was rung, she cracked.

A: People blamed the clapper. Too big, they said, too heavy, swung too hard. Others blamed a flaw in the casting.

N: What is certain is that she has been silent ever since. The largest voice in Toledo, and it spoke once.

...

N: If you go up, look down on the city from beside her. The river looping round the rock. The churches you have walked to today. And beneath you, this whole stone ship, built over a mosque, built over a church.

N: If you do not go up, stand in the garden and look at the tower. Somewhere near the top, behind the openings, the fat lady is hanging in the dark, cracked and silent.

## Walk on

N: When you are ready, play the closing track, stop ten, here in the cloister or wherever you are.
