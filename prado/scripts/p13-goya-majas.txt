# title: Stop 13. Goya: The Naked Maja and The Clothed Maja
# stop: 13
# room: 38

## A room behind a door

N: Stop thirteen. First floor, room thirty-eight. Look for two paintings side by side of the same young woman, lying on a green couch, hands behind her head. In one she is dressed. In the other she is not.

...

N: Imagine a palace in Madrid, and a door most guests never pass. Behind it, the most powerful man in Spain keeps the pictures no one is supposed to own.

N: He is Godoy, the queen's favourite, the man missing from the royal family portrait. In his secret room hangs a nude by Velázquez, a goddess seen from behind. And beside it hangs something far more dangerous.

## The woman who looks back

...

N: Look at the naked one first. Look at her eyes.

N: In this country, the Church frowned on painted nudes. The few that existed were goddesses, nymphs, figures from old myths, safely far away. This woman has no myth to hide behind. She is a real Spaniard of her own day. And she is not shy. She looks straight at you, calm, a little amused.

...

N: Stand with that for a moment. Who is looking at whom? Is she on show for you, or are you on show for her?

## Whose face

...

N: Now look at her face again. It sits oddly on the body, almost as if it belongs to someone else. For two hundred years, people have tried to put a name to it.

...

A: The great legend says she is the Duchess of Alba. She was the richest, most daring woman in Madrid. Goya painted her, and after her husband died, he stayed with her at her country estate. Gossip made them lovers, and the gossip made her the Maja. You will even hear that her descendants opened her tomb to prove the body was not hers. It is legend, and a delicious one.

N: The likelier answer is closer to home. Godoy had a young mistress named Pepita Tudó. Years later he married her. A private picture of the private man's own lover. But no one knows for certain. The Maja has kept her secret.

## The Inquisition knocks

...

N: Then the world turns. A mob brings Godoy down. His palace is stripped, his pictures listed, and the secret room is opened to strangers. Among them, the Church.

N: In eighteen fifteen, the Inquisition orders Goya to appear before it. Whose are these paintings? Why did you make them? For whom?

N: Goya is nearly seventy. The king who has just returned is hunting down men like him. This summons could ruin him.

...

N: And then the record goes silent. We do not know if he went, or what he said. We only know that he went on painting, and the Maja survived.

## Dressed for company

...

N: Now look at the clothed Maja. The same pose. The same gaze. But here the paint is quicker, looser, the jacket and sash dashed on.

A: You will often hear that the clothed Maja hung in front of the naked one, and that a hidden device slid it aside for special guests. It is told everywhere. No document proves it. But stand between the two, and you can see why the story was invented.

## Scandal by post

...

N: Her power to shock outlived them all. In nineteen thirty, Spain put the naked Maja on postage stamps. The United States banned letters that carried her. A woman painted for one man's locked room was now crossing oceans, and still causing trouble.

N: Look at her eyes one last time. She is still not embarrassed. You might be.

## Walk on

...

N: Now go down to the ground floor and follow the map to room sixty-four. That is stop fourteen.
