# title: Stop 6. Dürer: Self-portrait, and Adam and Eve
# stop: 6
# room: 55B

## The young man who wanted more

N: Stop six. Ground floor, room fifty-five B. Look for a small portrait of a young man with long golden curls, in black and white, a window behind him.

N: Picture a young man coming home over the Alps. He has been in Italy, and he has seen something he cannot forget. There, painters are treated like gentlemen. They talk with princes and scholars. Back home in Nuremberg, he is a craftsman. He belongs to a guild, like the man who mends your shoes.

N: His name is Albrecht Dürer. He is twenty-six. And he decides to paint the man he wants to be.

## Dressed for the part

...

N: Look at what he chose to wear. A striped cap with a tassel. A fine white jacket edged in black. A cloak thrown over one shoulder. And on his hands, soft leather gloves. Gloves said, these hands do not labour. It is a costume, and a claim.

...

N: Now look at his eyes. His body turns away, but his eyes come round to meet yours, cool and sure. Stand here a moment. Who is judging whom?

N: Below the window, find a line of writing in his own German. It says, I painted this from my own figure. I was twenty-six years old. And then his mark, a large A with a small D sheltering under it.

## A gift for a doomed king

...

N: Now follow the picture's journey, because it moves from king to king. More than a century later, his own city sent it to England, as a gift for Charles the First, a king who loved paintings more than almost anything.

N: Then the English turned on their king. Charles was put on trial, and beheaded. His enormous collection was sold off, piece by piece. Agents from across Europe came shopping. The King of Spain bought well, and Dürer's young gentleman came to Madrid.

N: The man who wanted to be seen among princes got his wish. He just had to wait for one to lose his head.

## The nudes that nearly burned

...

N: Now, if they are hanging nearby, find two tall narrow panels. A naked man and a naked woman, life-size, against black. Adam and Eve, painted in fifteen oh seven. If you cannot see them, check the map.

N: Dürer spent years measuring the human body, hunting for perfect proportion. Here he lets it go. Eve takes the apple from the serpent. Adam holds a branch of the tree. Look at their feet. They almost dance.

...

N: Their journey is a story of war. They hung in the castle in Prague, until a Swedish army sacked it and carried them north as loot. Years later, a queen of Sweden gave them away, to the King of Spain.

N: And in Madrid they became an embarrassment. Two naked bodies, in the most Catholic court in Europe. They were hidden in rooms kept for nudes, behind closed doors.

...

N: Then came a pious new king, Charles the Third. He looked at the royal nudes and decided they were indecent. They should be destroyed.

N: His court painter, Anton Raphael Mengs, persuaded him otherwise. These were works of genius, he argued. The king gave way. But Adam and Eve were locked away, out of sight, for decades. Even after they reached this museum, they waited years before the public was allowed to see them.

A: You will hear that the bonfire was all but built, and the panels came within days of the flames. That is legend, polished by retelling. The order to destroy them is real. The bonfire may not be.

...

N: Look at them once more. Shame is what they were about to learn, in the story. For a century, it was what everyone made them carry.

## Walk on

...

N: That ends the ground floor. Now go up to the first floor, following the signs and the museum map. El Greco is next. That is stop seven.
