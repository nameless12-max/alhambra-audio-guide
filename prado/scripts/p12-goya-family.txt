# title: Stop 12. Goya: The Family of Charles the Fourth
# stop: 12
# room: 32

## The man who could not hear

N: Stop twelve. First floor, room thirty-two. Look for the biggest canvas in the room, a royal family standing in one long glittering row.

...

N: Picture a palace in spring. Servants hurry through the halls. One by one, princes and princesses sit for a painter, and he sketches each face fast, in oil, on small canvases. They talk to him. He does not answer. He cannot hear a word they say.

N: His name is Goya. A few years before, a sudden illness nearly killed him. He lived, but the world went silent, and it stayed silent for the rest of his life. A deaf man, in a court that runs on whispers.

N: And yet here he is, at the very top. The king has just made him First Court Painter. Now he must paint the whole family, in one picture, and every one of them will look at the result. What will a man who cannot hear the flattery around him choose to show?

## The woman at the centre

...

N: Find the king. Big, solid, in brown, his chest heavy with medals, standing a little right of centre. He is not the middle of this picture.

N: Now look to the true centre. A woman in a silver dress, bare arms, a small boy holding her hand. She is the queen, María Luisa. Everyone at court knew where power lived in this family. Goya has simply put it where it belonged.

N: And beside her should stand the one man who is missing. Manuel Godoy. A handsome young guardsman who rose, through her favour, to rule Spain.

...

A: Madrid whispered that Godoy was the queen's lover. Pamphlets said it. Cartoons drew it. The king, they said, was the last to know. It was never proved, and it hardly mattered. Everyone believed it.

## A face that turns away

...

N: Now look to the far left. A young man in blue steps forward. He is the heir to the throne. Beside him a young woman has turned her head away, as if she cannot bear to be seen.

N: Nobody knows who she is. One idea is that she stands for the prince's future bride, not yet chosen. A place kept open in the family, with no face to fill it. Stay with her a moment. In a room full of people showing themselves off, she is the only one hiding.

## The painter in the dark

...

N: Now look behind the prince, into the shadows at the back. A man stands at a huge canvas, half lost in the gloom, looking straight out at you.

N: It is Goya. And you have seen this before. A few rooms ago, in Las Meninas, Velázquez painted himself at his easel beside the king's daughter. Goya loved Velázquez above all painters. Here he takes the same place. But Velázquez stands in warm light, among friends. Goya stands in the dark, watching.

## The grocer and the lottery

...

N: So, flattery or mockery? Look at the faces. The queen's is sharp and hard. The king's is kindly, and empty.

...

A: The famous jibe is usually credited to the French writer Théophile Gautier. He is said to have called them the corner grocer and his wife, just after winning the lottery. It is a wicked line, and it stuck.

N: Yet the family seems to have been pleased. Goya kept his post. Perhaps that is the most unsettling thing of all. He painted them exactly as they were, and they liked it.

## The glitter goes out

...

N: Look one last time at all that silk and all those diamonds. Within eight years, every bit of it is gone. Godoy is toppled by a mob. The king gives up his crown. Napoleon lures father and son to France and keeps the throne for himself.

N: The painter in the shadows will still be here, watching, when the lights go out.
