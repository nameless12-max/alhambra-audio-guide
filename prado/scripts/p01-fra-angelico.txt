# title: Stop 1. Fra Angelico: The Annunciation
# stop: 1
# room: 56B

## A friar at his altar

N: Stop one. Ground floor, room fifty-six B. If it has moved, check the map. Look for a square altarpiece blazing with gold, an angel in pink kneeling before a woman in blue.

N: Imagine a hilltop convent above Florence. A friar kneels at a side altar before dawn. The man who painted this picture was one of those friars. He painted it for his own brothers, to pray in front of every day of their lives. He was not painting for a museum. He was painting for men who would look at it for the rest of their lives.

N: If you have been to Florence, you may have climbed the stairs in the convent of San Marco. The same friar painted the walls there, and at the top of those stairs another angel greets another Mary.

...

## The moment before

N: Now look at the two figures. The angel has just landed. His wings are still up. He bends forward, arms crossed on his chest. And Mary bends towards him, with her arms crossed the same way.

N: Stay with her face. Nothing has happened yet. In a moment she will say yes, and in the story the friars believed, the whole world turns on that word. But the painter stops just before it. She is listening.

...

N: Now find the line of gold light. It falls from the top left corner, straight across the picture. Ride down it with your eye. A tiny white dove is sliding along it, towards her.

## Who is running away

N: But there is someone else in this picture, and they are not happy. Look to the far left. Two small figures are being driven out of the garden by an angel.

N: Adam and Eve. Why put them here, at the edge of this quiet room? Because for the friars, this was one story. On the left, the first people lose paradise. Under the arches, a young woman says yes, and it begins to be won back. The fall and the rescue, side by side, read from left to right.

...

## Sold for a bell tower

N: This picture should still be on that hill. But early in the sixteen hundreds, the convent needed money. The story is that the friars sold it to build a new bell tower. One account says it was a gift instead. Either way, it left Italy for the court of Spain, as a present for the most powerful man in the kingdom after the king.

N: It spent the next centuries in a closed convent of nuns in Madrid, seen only by them. When the museum finally brought it here, it was the first time ordinary people could stand where you are now.

N: Even here, it had grown dim. Centuries of old varnish had turned the blue grey and the gold dull. For the museum's two hundredth birthday, restorers cleaned it, slowly, by hand. The blue of her cloak came back. The pink of the angel's robe came back. The gold began to burn again. It became the heart of a great exhibition on the friar who painted it.

...

## What it is worth

N: How much is it worth? It will never be sold. But in twenty sixteen the Prado bought a much smaller Fra Angelico, a little Virgin with a pomegranate, from a Spanish duke. The price was eighteen million euros. This altarpiece is far larger, and far more famous.

...

N: Before you go, look once more at the gap between the angel and the woman. A hand's breadth of air. What would you have said?

N: Walk on now to stop two, the other Mona Lisa.
