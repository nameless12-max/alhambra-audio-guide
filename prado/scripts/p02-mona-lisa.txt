## The woman in the dark

N: Stop two. Ground floor, among the Italian Renaissance paintings. Sources give room forty-nine or fifty-six B, so check the map. Look for a small portrait of a woman with folded hands, before a blue landscape of rivers and rocks.

N: For perhaps two centuries, she sat in darkness. Her whole background was painted solid black. Visitors to the Prado walked straight past her. Just another old copy of a famous face. Nobody knew who made it. Some thought a Flemish painter, far from Italy.

N: Hold that black in your mind. Because the question is, what was underneath it?

...

## The wrong wood

N: A few years before the answer came, the Louvre in Paris asked to borrow her for a Leonardo exhibition. So the Prado's experts looked at her properly, for the first time.

N: The first surprise was the wood. Not oak, the wood of Flemish painters. Walnut, a wood Leonardo often used himself.

N: Then they looked beneath the paint, with infrared light. Under the face, the painter's drawing had been corrected. He had changed his mind as he worked. And every change followed the changes Leonardo made to his own Mona Lisa.

N: Think about what that means. A copyist working from a finished picture has nothing to correct. This painter was correcting along with the master. He was sitting in the same room, at the same time, painting the same woman.

## Under the black

...

N: Then the restorers began to take the black away. Slowly. And out of the dark came a world. Now find it. Look behind her shoulders. Winding roads. A bridge. Water. Blue mountains melting into haze. The same dream country that stands behind the woman in Paris.

...

N: Now look at her face. The original sits under old yellow varnish, and it has never been fully cleaned. So she looks brown and ancient. Here, the skin is fresh. The sleeves are a warm red. Many people say she looks younger, like someone you might meet. This is the closest you can come to seeing what Leonardo's studio saw.

## The twin goes to Paris

N: When the museum announced the discovery, papers around the world called her the Mona Lisa's twin. Before she left for Paris, so many people crowded in to see her that her room was too small to hold them. The woman nobody had looked at for centuries had a queue.

N: In Paris she was under one roof with her sister, the most famous painting in the world. When the original went to New York in nineteen sixty-two, it was valued for insurance at a hundred million dollars. No painting has had a higher insurance valuation. That was the sister. This one spent those years hidden in black.

## The boy who stole

N: So who painted her? Perhaps one of two pupils. One was a young nobleman. The other was Salaì, a boy who came to live with Leonardo at ten years old. In his notes, Leonardo called him a thief, a liar, obstinate, a glutton. He kept him for decades, all the same.

...

A: And here is the rumour you will hear. That Salaì was more than a pupil. That this portrait, painted at his master's side, was a kind of love letter. It is a romantic guess. Nothing proves whose hand this was.

...

N: Stand back. Two women, painted side by side, in one workshop. One became the most famous face on earth. The other was buried in black, and waited. Which one is looking at you?
