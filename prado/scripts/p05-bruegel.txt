# title: Stop 5. Bruegel: The Triumph of Death
# stop: 5
# room: 55A

## The day nobody is spared

N: Stop five. Ground floor, room fifty-five A. Look for a wide brown panel under a burning sky, its whole landscape crawling with skeletons.

N: Imagine a city where the plague comes back every few years. Where the bell tolls, and the cart comes round, and you count who is missing from church. That was the world of Pieter Bruegel. Around fifteen sixty-two, he painted what everyone there feared. Not one death. Everyone's death, on the same afternoon.

N: So here is the question to carry with you. In this whole crowded picture, is there anyone who escapes?

## The army

...

N: Start on the right. An army of skeletons marches in ranks, behind shields made from coffin lids. On the left, a skeleton on a starved horse swings a scythe, driving the living before it. Behind him, more skeletons ring a bell.

...

N: Now follow where the living are being driven. In the middle right stands a huge box, shaped like a coffin, painted with crosses. Its door is open. People are crowding into it, pushed from behind. It is a trap, and they are running into it.

## The rich and the holy

...

N: Now the lower left corner. A king lies dying in red, still in his crown. Beside him, barrels of gold. A skeleton is already helping itself to his money. Another holds up an hourglass in front of his face. The sand has run out.

N: All his life he could buy anything. He cannot buy one more minute.

...

N: Just above him, a skeleton in a cardinal's red hat is steering a real cardinal to his end. The church cannot save its own princes.

## The dog and the child

...

N: Now slow down. Look at the ground in the lower left, near the cart. A woman lies dead, a baby in her arms. A thin dog has come to sniff at the child.

N: Nobody is fighting here. Nobody is screaming. That small, quiet horror is easy to miss, and hard to forget.

...

## The lovers

...

N: Now cross to the lower right corner. This is the answer to the question.

N: Two lovers sit apart from the slaughter. He plays a lute. She follows the music in a book. Their heads are close. They have noticed nothing at all. They are the only people in the painting who look happy.

...

N: Now look just behind them. A skeleton is playing along on its own instrument. Keeping time.

N: So does anyone escape? No. But Bruegel gives two people the one mercy left. They have not seen it coming yet.

...

N: Stand with them a moment. If the music stopped now, would you want to know?

## A Bruegel in the family

...

N: The Prado has a second Bruegel story, and a happier one. For centuries, a noble Spanish family owned a big, worn canvas of a village feast. Peasants crowd around a giant barrel, holding up jugs and bowls, scrambling for the new wine on Saint Martin's Day.

N: The family brought it to the Prado to be studied and restored, with an option for the museum to buy. Experts studied it for months. Then, under examination, fragments of a signature appeared. Bruegel.

N: In two thousand ten, the Spanish government confirmed the purchase for seven million euros, the figure at which it had been valued. It is painted in glue on linen, so fragile that almost nothing like it survives. If The Wine of Saint Martin's Day is on display today, check the map. It may hang near this room.

N: The Triumph of Death has no price at all. It will never be sold.

...

N: Walk on to room fifty-five B, for Albrecht Dürer. Check the map. That is stop six.
