# title: Stop 3. Rogier van der Weyden: The Descent from the Cross
# stop: 3
# room: 58

## The archers' chapel

N: Stop three. Ground floor, room fifty-eight. If it has moved, check the map. Look for a wide panel on a flat gold ground, with ten figures, almost life size, lowering a pale dead body from the cross.

N: Imagine a small chapel outside the walls of a Flemish town. It belongs to a guild of crossbowmen, the citizens who drill with bows to defend their city. They have saved, and they want the best altarpiece money can buy. They go to the painter Rogier van der Weyden.

N: And they want the world to know it was theirs. So where did he hide their name?

...

N: Look into the top corners, in the gold carving. Find the tiny crossbows. There they are.

N: Now look again at the body of Christ. See how it bends, in one long curve, like a drawn bow. And below him, his mother has fainted. Her body makes the same curve. The son dies. The mother collapses into his shape.

## Grief you can count

...

N: Now step close, as close as the guard allows. Look at the faces. Find the tears.

N: They are clear and round. They run down the cheeks and catch the light, one by one. You could count them. Look at the faces around the mother. Look at the man holding the feet. Every person in this box is breaking in a different way.

N: Nearly six hundred years later, people still stop dead here. A great historian of art wrote that a single painted tear held everything the Italians envied in these Flemish painters.

...

N: Now look down, near the foot of the cross. Find the skull. It is Adam, the first man, buried, so the story went, right where the cross would stand. The first death, and this one.

## The queen's bargain

N: About a hundred years after it was painted, a very powerful woman came to see it. She was the emperor's sister, and she ruled these lands for him. She wanted it.

N: So the archers made a deal. They gave up their masterpiece. In return, the queen gave them a painted copy and a new organ for their chapel. A copy and an organ, for this. That was the price.

## The ship that sank

...

A: Now a legend, and a good one. They say the panel was sent by sea to Spain, and the ship went down. All seemed lost. But the crate was packed so well that it floated, and the painting came ashore almost unharmed. A Flemish writer told it a few generations later. It may be only a story.

...

## The king who kept it

N: When the queen died, her nephew inherited it. He was the most powerful king in Europe, King Philip of Spain. He hung it in his chapel, and later in his great monastery palace in the mountains, El Escorial. It stayed there for more than three and a half centuries.

N: Then came the Civil War. It went with the Prado's treasures, in the lorries to Valencia, and on to Geneva. When it came home in nineteen thirty-nine, it came here, to the museum. It will never be sold. A queen paid for it with an organ. Today no amount of money could buy it.

...

N: Stand still for a moment. No sky. No landscape. Only gold, and these people, and their grief. Have you ever stood beside someone like the mother here?

N: Walk on now to stop four, Hieronymus Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights, in room fifty-six A.
