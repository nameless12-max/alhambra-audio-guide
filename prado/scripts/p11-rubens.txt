# title: Stop 11. Rubens: The Three Graces
# stop: 11
# room: 29

## A picture nobody was meant to see

N: Stop eleven. First floor, room twenty-nine. Look for three naked women standing in a ring, arms around each other, under a garland of flowers in a green landscape.

...

N: Before you look closely, here is a question to hold. Why did a king of Spain order this painting burned? And how did it survive?

## The old man and the girl

N: Picture Antwerp, a rich port city in Flanders. A painter in his fifties, the most famous in Europe, is grieving. His wife of many years has died. He is rich, honoured, knighted by kings. And he is alone.

N: Then he does something that sets the city talking. He marries again. She is a merchant's daughter, and she is sixteen years old. Her name is Helena.

N: He does not hide how he feels. For the last ten years of his life he paints her again and again. As a goddess. As a saint. As herself, laughing, with their children.

...

N: Now look at the woman on the left. Fair hair, soft round face, turning towards her sisters. Many believe this is Helena. An old man's young wife, painted into a myth, welcomed by the goddesses as one of their own.

N: Look at the hands. Each woman holds another, lightly, at the shoulder, the arm, the waist. Nobody grips. Look at the skin, the faint pink of blood under it. This is not marble. This is someone he touched.

## Kept behind a door

N: This was not painted for a patron. Rubens kept it for himself, in his own house. It was still there when he died.

A: The story is often told that Helena, after his death, was ashamed of the intimate pictures of her, and wanted some of them destroyed. It is gossip, and it usually attaches to another painting of her. But you can see why the rumour clung.

N: His belongings were sold. The king of Spain, who adored his work, bought this one. And so it came to Madrid.

## The king who wanted it burned

N: Now the answer to the question.

...

N: More than a century later, a different king of Spain looked at the royal nudes and saw sin. He ordered them burned. This picture was on the list.

N: It was saved by being hidden. It was locked away with other nudes in a closed room at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts, where only a few were allowed to see it. The fire never came.

...

N: Stand back now and see the three of them whole. A painting one man made out of love, and another man wanted to destroy out of shame. Which of them do you think was looking more honestly?

## The painter who carried secrets

N: Rubens knew this court well. He had come to Madrid as a diplomat, on a secret mission to make peace with England.

N: Here he met the king's young painter, Velázquez, not yet thirty. The famous visitor kept his distance from most court painters. Not this one. The two went out together to the royal monastery to study its great Venetian paintings. Soon after, the young man left for Italy. Many believe Rubens told him to go.

## A god who eats his children

N: The museum also has a darker Rubens. Saturn, the god who ate his own children so none could overthrow him. It is listed in room seventy-nine, so check the map.

N: Rubens shows a powerful old god, stars above his head, tearing into a baby's chest. It is horrible, but it is still a myth.

N: Keep it in your mind. Before the end of this tour, Goya will show you the same god. And Goya's will not let you sleep.

...
