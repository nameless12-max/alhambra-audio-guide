## Key facts and sources

Bosch, Garden (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_of_Earthly_Delights):
- Seen by Antonio de Beatis in the Nassau palace, Brussels, 1517.
- Passed to William of Orange. Confiscated by the Duke of Alba, 1568. Went to Fernando de Toledo, Alba's illegitimate son. Philip II bought it at auction 1591, sent to El Escorial 1593. Prado 1939.
- Shutters: grisaille, creation, inscription Psalm 33:9 "Ipse dixit et facta sunt".
- Sigüenza 1605 defence. Fraenger 1947 Adamite theory, script places it in the "nineteen forties" and in the A voice as widely rejected.
- Butt music transcribed 2014 by a student (Amelia Hamrick, Oklahoma Christian University), widely reported. Name not used in script.
- Tree man self-portrait: speculative, A voice.
- Owl in the fountain of the left panel, and a man embracing a large owl in the lower left of the centre panel: from general art-historical description, standard. Worth a visual check.
- Table of Seven Deadly Sins in Philip II's rooms at El Escorial, and attribution doubts: commonly reported, not checked against a primary source this session.

Bruegel, Triumph of Death (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Triumph_of_Death):
- c. 1562, oil on panel. In the Prado since 1827.
- Restored 2016 to 2018 with Iberdrola sponsorship (https://www.fundacioniberdrolaespana.org/en/press-room/2018-news/prado-museum-exhibits-the-triumph-of-the-death-of-bruegel-after-its-restoration). Exact start year of restoration inferred from "two-year restoration" reports.
- "By the seventeen hundreds in the royal collection": from memory of Prado provenance (Elizabeth Farnese, La Granja). Not verified this session. Low risk, but check.
- Political reading in A voice.

Bruegel, Wine of Saint Martin's Day (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wine_of_Saint_Martin%27s_Day):
- Identified 2010 during restoration at the Prado, signature fragments found. Glue tempera on linen. Bought by the Prado, El País headline "por siete millones" (euros), below market value. Earlier owners: Medinaceli line.

Dürer, Self-portrait (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-portrait_at_26):
- Nuremberg gave it to Charles I in 1636 (one Prado entry says 1632, sources disagree, script uses 1636). Acquired by Philip IV after Charles's execution, at the Commonwealth sale. Wikipedia gives 1654.
- Inscription "Das malt ich nach meiner gestalt / Ich was sex und zwenzig jor alt / Albrecht Dürer": from memory, standard, paraphrased in script.

Dürer, Adam and Eve (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_and_Eve_(D%C3%BCrer); https://www.museodelprado.es/en/whats-on/exhibition/special-display-adam-and-eve-by-durer-following/45e26a43-8be9-49ba-bcd3-f2935725a9cc):
- 1507. Rudolf II in Prague. Looted by Swedes 1648. Christina gave to Philip IV, 1654.
- 1762 Charles III wanted royal nudes destroyed. Mengs persuaded him. Stored at the Academia de San Fernando. Prado 1827, not shown publicly until 1830s (1833 or 1838, sources differ).
- Restoration completed 2010.
- Correction to the brief: the gift to Charles I of England concerns the Self-portrait, not Adam and Eve.
- "Ordered burned on a bonfire" detail kept in A voice as unconfirmed.
